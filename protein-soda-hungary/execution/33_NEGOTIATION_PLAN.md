# 33 — NEGOTIATION PLAN

**How to cut MOQ and upfront cash. Ranked by likely savings.**

Your position is weak on volume and strong on simplicity. Trade the second for the first.

---

## WHAT YOU ACTUALLY HAVE TO TRADE

A co-packer's real costs are **changeovers, cleaning and complexity** — not liquid. You are unusually cheap to serve:

- **One SKU, one flavour** — no changeovers mid-run
- **No printed cans** — you use their stock brite cans
- **No artwork approval loops** — sleeves are applied separately
- **Flexible on date** — you can fill a cancellation slot
- **Flexible on format** — standard or sleek, whichever they already run

**Say all of this in the first email.** It costs nothing and it reframes you from "annoying small order" to "easy filler between big jobs."

---

## THE TACTICS, RANKED BY LIKELY SAVING

### 1. Ask to ride a cancellation or changeover slot ⭐ biggest single lever
**Saving: potentially 30–50% of the setup fee**

Co-packers have cancelled runs and gaps between jobs. A line already cleaned and running a similar acidic soft drink can take your 1,000 litres at marginal cost.

> *"We are completely flexible on timing. If you ever have a cancellation or a gap after a similar non-alcoholic acidic product, we would take that slot at short notice at a reduced rate."*

**Why it works:** you convert idle capacity into revenue for them. Their alternative is zero.

---

### 2. Use their stock can, not a printed one
**Saving: enormous — this is the difference between possible and impossible**

Printed cans require **150,000–250,000 unit** minimums. Rhodius — the German canner with a startup-friendly reputation — publishes a **250,000 can** minimum `SEARCH-VERIFIED`. Can makers themselves sell by the truckload.

**Never ask for printed cans at this volume.** Blank can + shrink sleeve is not a compromise, it is the only route.

---

### 3. Go collagen-first, whey-second
**Saving: opens the entire co-packer pool + ~39 HUF/can**

If whey triggers a Reg. 853/2004 dairy approval requirement or an allergen changeover surcharge, **most can fillers are disqualified or expensive**. Collagen needs neither.

> *"If whey protein creates an allergen changeover or approval issue at your site, we can reformulate to collagen peptides only. Please quote both."*

**Always ask for both quotes.** The delta tells you exactly what whey costs you, and gives you a live fallback if the whey market stays short.

---

### 4. Ask for turnkey ingredient sourcing
**Saving: avoids ingredient MOQ traps worth several hundred euro**

If you buy ingredients yourself you may be forced into 25 kg minimums of things you need 2 kg of. A co-packer buying at scale absorbs that.

> *"Please quote turnkey including all raw materials. If we must supply any ingredient, tell us which and in what minimum quantity."*

---

### 5. Negotiate the deposit down to 30%
**Saving: ~€350 of week-11 cashflow — see `finance/21_CASHFLOW.md`**

MONYO publishes 50% `SEARCH-VERIFIED`. Assume it is the norm and push.

> *"Would 30% on order and 70% on delivery be acceptable? As a new company our cashflow is tight until the first stock sells, and we would rather commit to a second order sooner than tie up capital now."*

**Offer something back:** a faster second order, or payment on dispatch rather than on receipt.

---

### 6. Ask them to quote DDP Budapest
**Saving: removes a hidden cost and a hidden risk**

One number, one counterparty, no freight forwarder to manage, no surprise on arrival.

---

### 7. Ask what THEIR minimum batch is in litres, not cans
**Saving: reframes the whole negotiation**

The real constraint is usually **tank volume**, not willingness. 1,700 cans is only **561 litres** — below most syrup-room minimums.

> *"What is the smallest batch your mixing system can run? If your minimum is 2,000 litres, we would rather produce 6,000 cans in one run than 1,700 in an uneconomic one — provided the per-can price reflects the larger volume."*

⚠️ **But watch the DRS threshold.** 6,000 cans crosses ~5,000 and triggers full deposit-system compliance. If a co-packer's floor is 2,000 L, either find a smaller one or deliberately decide to cross the threshold with the artwork prepared for it. **Do not stumble across it by accident.**

---

### 8. Bundle the second order into the first negotiation
**Saving: 10–20% on per-can price**

> *"Our first run is 1,700 cans. If it sells through, our second order within 6 months would be 10,000–25,000. Could you price the first run on the assumption that it is the start of a relationship rather than a one-off?"*

**Do not fake this.** If you say it, mean it.

---

### 9. Accept their existing flavour system
**Saving: the entire recipe development fee**

Many co-packers have proven lemon–lime bases. Using one removes development cost, development time and formulation risk.

> *"Do you have an existing citrus or lemon–lime flavour base we could build on? We are not attached to a bespoke flavour for the first run."*

---

### 10. One SKU. Not two.
**Saving: roughly half the total project cost**

Two SKUs = two runs, two artworks, two GTINs, two minimum ingredient buys, and a 1,700-can batch split into two unsellable halves.

**The caffeine SKU waits for batch 2.**

---

## WHAT NOT TO NEGOTIATE

| Never trade away | Why |
|---|---|
| **Certificate of Analysis per batch** | Your only evidence the product is what the label says. Non-negotiable |
| **Written specification** | Verbal specs produce disputes you cannot win |
| **Best-before date printed on can** | Legally required |
| **Their food safety certification** | If they have none, walk away |
| **Knowing the actual filling site address** | You are legally responsible for the product |

---

## THE ONE THING THAT BEATS ALL TEN TACTICS

> **Show up with signed Letters of Intent.**

A co-packer treats "I want 1,700 cans" and "I have 8 gyms committed to 1,000 cans and here are their signatures" as two completely different customers.

It costs you nothing and it changes the conversation from *whether* they will serve you to *how*.

**This is also, not coincidentally, exactly what Ölgerðin did** — they co-developed UPP with Iceland's largest gym chain and had guaranteed day-one distribution before producing anything.

---

## SCRIPT: when they say the MOQ is 15,000 and you want 2,000

```
Thank you — that's helpful to know.

I understand 15,000 is your standard minimum, and I'm not going to
pretend our first order is attractive on volume.

What I can offer instead is that we're an unusually simple job:
one SKU, one flavour, your stock brite cans, no printed can artwork,
no changeover mid-run, and complete flexibility on timing.

Three questions:

1. Would you run a smaller first batch if we accepted a higher price
   per can and took a cancellation or gap slot at short notice?

2. What is your minimum batch in LITRES? If it's tank-limited rather
   than policy, we'd rather size our order to your tank than to a
   round number.

3. If neither works, is there a smaller filler you'd recommend?
   We'd rather come back to you at 25,000 units having launched
   successfully elsewhere than not launch at all.

Either way, thank you for the straight answer.
```

> **Question 3 is the important one.** Co-packers know every other co-packer. A referral from one is worth more than ten cold emails, and asking for it costs you nothing but the order you were not going to get anyway.
