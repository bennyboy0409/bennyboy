## 1. WHO YOU ARE IN LAW

You buy filled cans from a co-packer, store them, and sell them to gyms and direct to consumers. In EU food law that is **distribution**. Reg. 178/2002 Art. 3(2) defines a food business as any undertaking carrying out any activity at any stage of production, **processing and distribution** of food; Art. 3(3) defines the food business operator as the person responsible for ensuring food law is met within the business under their control.

**→ You are an FBO in your own right. Using a co-packer SPLITS responsibility, it does not TRANSFER it.**
Status: LIKELY — model knowledge, not verified 2026-08-16.

### 1.1 The obligation split — memorise this table before your first co-packer call

| Obligation | Co-packer (gyarto) | **YOU** (brand owner + distributor / forgalmazo) | If co-packer is in another EU state |
|---|---|---|---|

| Registration of **your storage establishment** | — | **YOURS. Non-transferable.** | Unchanged — still yours, in Hungary |
| HACCP for the **filling process**, thermal validation, shelf-life study, micro testing | **Theirs to write.** | **Yours to obtain and hold on file.** Put it in the supply agreement. | Theirs |
| **GHP/HACCP for storage and distribution** | — | **YOURS** | Unchanged |
| **Food information on the label — Reg. 1169/2011 Art. 8(1)** | Only if sold under *their* name | **YOURS — your name is on the can** | **Still yours.** Cross-border co-packing does not move this |

### 1.2 Art. 8(1) FIC stated plainly, because it is the one that surprises founders

> **The operator whose name and address appears on the can is the food business operator responsible for the food information.** The allergen declaration (**milk** — you have whey), the nutrition table, the Hungarian-language requirement, the net quantity, the lot mark, the durability date, the "magas fehérjetartalmú" claim — **all your legal exposure, even though someone else printed it.**

The co-packer will offer to "handle the artwork". **They are not liable for it; you are.** The supply agreement must therefore require them to (a) supply true, tested nutritional and allergen data, (b) apply **exactly** the artwork you approve with no substitutions, and (c) indemnify you for deviations. Indemnity wording: **NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION (lawyer).**

**Sharpening this from 2026-12-09:** Directive (EU) 2024/2853, the new Product Liability Directive, makes the **own-brand seller liable as if it were the manufacturer**. Transposition deadline **2026-12-09** — under four months from today. Status: **CONFIRMED** (`https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2853/oj/eng`, retrieved live 2026-08-16 by workstream A1). **Your batch-1 launch lands on or after that date. Plan on being treated as the manufacturer for liability purposes.**

### 3.1 Which authority does what

- **NEBIH (Nemzeti Elelmiszerlanc-biztonsagi Hivatal)** — national authority. Operates **FELIR (Elelmiszerlanc-Felugyeleti Informacios Rendszer)**, the national register of food-chain operators, and administers the supervision fee.

**→ You file with the jarasi hivatal for the location of your storage establishment. NEBIH's system issues the FELIR number.**

## 4. LEGAL VEHICLE — THE MINOR CONSTRAINT, RESTATED FOR THIS FILE

The food-chain notification is filed **by a legal person or a registered sole trader**, not by an individual. That collides with the founder's age:

- **A minor CANNOT be an egyeni vallalkozo** — Evectv. 2009. evi CXV. **3. § (2)**.
- **A minor CANNOT be an ugyvezeto** — Ptk. **3:22 § (1)**.
- **No parental consent cures either.**
- **A minor CAN be a Kft. tag** through a torvenyes kepviselo, and **from 16 can be an employee**.

**→ CHEAPEST ROUTE: use an existing adult family company and add TEAOR 4634 (+ 4725 for D2C). That company becomes the FBO, holds the FELIR number, and its name goes on the can under Art. 8(1).**
**Fallback:** father as a **SECONDARY-occupation** egyeni vallalkozo. **Do NOT set up a full-time EV — 101,682 HUF/month ≈ EUR 280/month with zero revenue.** Over a 6-month pre-launch period that is **610,092 HUF ≈ EUR 1,681** — **more than the founder's entire personal capital of EUR 1,000.** DERIVED: 101,682 × 6 = 610,092; 610,092 / 363 = 1,680.7.

**If the EV route is ever taken:** the registration portal changed on **2026-02-19** to `https://ugyfelportal.nav.gov.hu/vallalkozoi-ugyseged/main`. **nyilvantarto.hu closed to new applications on 2026-03-01** — any guide or forum post telling you to use nyilvantarto.hu is out of date.

## 6. STORING THE CANS AT HOME OR IN A GARAGE

**This is the biggest gap between what founders assume and what the law says, and the most commercially significant open item in this file.**

### 6.1 What is reasonably certain

- **Reg. 852/2004 Art. 6(2) requires every ESTABLISHMENT under your control to be notified for registration.** A room or garage used to hold trade stock **is an establishment**. It is **not exempt because the property is residential.** Status: LIKELY.
- **Annex II of Reg. 852/2004 applies**: premises clean and in good repair, laid out to avoid contamination, protected against pests, suitable temperature, no contamination risk from non-food items. Status: LIKELY.
- Storing **unopened, hermetically sealed, shelf-stable cans** is close to the lowest-risk food activity that exists. The requirements that really bite (potable water supply, hand-wash basins, wipeable surface finishes, changing facilities) are aimed at open-food handling and are expected to apply in a **reduced, proportionate** form. Reg. 852/2004 is explicitly a flexible instrument. Status: LIKELY.

### 6.2 What is genuinely unknown

| Is a dry warehouse for shelf-stable packaged drinks **notification-only** or **approval-required** under 57/2010 FVM r.? | **UNKNOWN.** Expected notification-only — **but see the next row.** |

### 6.3 Do these three things regardless of the answer — all free

1. **Stock off the floor** on pallets or shelving, **away from chemicals, fuel, paint, garden products and anything else a garage normally holds.** Cross-contamination from co-stored non-food items is the single most likely inspection finding in a garage.
2. **Control temperature and light.** A Hungarian garage swings far outside normal ambient shelf-life validation conditions. **The co-packer's stability data will specify a storage range. Exceeding it voids the shelf life printed on the can — and that best-before date is YOUR Art. 8(1) statement.** For ARCH F this is a product risk as much as a legal one: whey at pH 3.2 held hot will haze and the drink will look spoiled on a gym shelf.
3. **Keep the §8 stock records from day one.** A spreadsheet. Free. It is the first thing an inspector asks for.

### 6.4 If the home-warehouse route is blocked

**Do NOT rent a warehouse — that is fatal on a EUR 2,000–2,600 batch-1 budget.** The two low-cost escapes:

- **Have the co-packer hold the finished stock and dispatch on your instruction.** Converts a fixed cost and a registration burden into a per-unit cost.
- **Use a 3PL that already holds its own food-establishment registration.**

**→ ADD THIS QUESTION TO EVERY CO-PACKER RFQ (Opre Label, Bubee, MONYO, Sajatital, Private Label Drink): "Can you hold finished stock and dispatch on our instruction, and at what cost per pallet per month?"** For 1,700 cans this is roughly one pallet — a question worth asking before you pay a deposit.

## 7. HACCP AND GHP FOR A DISTRIBUTOR

### 7.1 Yes, you need something — but not a full HACCP plan

**Reg. 852/2004 Art. 5(1)** requires FBOs to put in place, implement and maintain permanent procedures based on **HACCP principles**, at all stages after primary production — **including storage and distribution. There is no blanket exemption for distributors.** Status: LIKELY.

**But** the regulation is explicitly proportionate. For a sealed, shelf-stable can under ambient storage there are **few or arguably no critical control points at the distribution stage.** The recognised route is:

- **Prerequisite programmes / Good Hygiene Practice (Jo Higieniai Gyakorlat, GHP)** as the backbone, plus
- **a simplified, hazard-analysis-based document** rather than a full 7-principle, 12-step HACCP plan with CCPs.

**Art. 5(5)** lets Member States facilitate implementation via **national guides to good practice**. Hungary publishes a **national GHP guide for trade/commerce (kereskedelem)**. **Adopting and adapting that guide is the intended low-cost compliance route for exactly this business.** Status: LIKELY.

| Question | Answer | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Is the national guide sufficient on its own? | **No — it is a template, not a filed document.** You must adopt it, adapt it to your actual premises and product, and keep the resulting records. A guide sitting unread is not compliance. | LIKELY |
| Where is it published? | Expected on `portal.nebih.gov.hu`. **Exact URL and document title UNKNOWN — blocked 2026-08-16** | — |
| Cost of the guide | Expected **0 HUF / EUR 0** (national guides are normally free downloads) | LIKELY — verify |
| Cost of a HACCP/GHP consultant in Hungary | **UNKNOWN — QUOTE REQUIRED.** No Hungarian published price list was retrievable. **No number is stated here on purpose.** | — |

**Budget stance: self-drafting from the free national GHP guide is a legitimate route for a storage-only operation and is what a EUR 2,000–2,600 batch budget implies. It is a weekend of work, not a purchase.**

### 7.2 What your GHP/HACCP document must contain — drafting checklist

Derived from Annex II of Reg. 852/2004 and standard practice. **Offered as a drafting checklist, not a verified regulatory list.**

1. Business identification, **FELIR number**, establishment address, scope, responsible person
2. **Product description**: 330 ml hermetically sealed aluminium can, ambient shelf-stable, **pH 3.20 ± 0.10**, **2.2 volumes CO2**, thermal process per co-packer specification, **allergen: MILK (whey)**, declared shelf life
3. **Flow diagram**: co-packer → inbound transport → goods-in check → storage → picking → outbound transport → gym/consumer
4. **Hazard analysis at each step.** For sealed cans the realistic hazards are **can/seam integrity damage, temperature abuse, pest activity on outer packaging, contamination from co-stored non-food items, and stock-rotation failure (expired product sold)** — not microbial growth inside the sealed can
5. **Goods-in acceptance criteria**: batch/lot number recorded, best-before recorded, **dented/leaking/blown cans rejected**, vehicle condition noted
6. **Storage controls**: temperature range, off-floor, segregation from chemicals, **FEFO (first-expired-first-out)**
7. Pest control arrangements
8. Cleaning schedule
9. Personal hygiene and training records — **yes, even for one person**
10. **Traceability procedure (§8) and withdrawal/recall procedure (§9) attached as annexes**
11. Non-conforming product handling: quarantine location, disposal records
12. Record retention rules and review date

## 8. TRACEABILITY — WHAT YOU MUST ACTUALLY KEEP

**Reg. 178/2002 Art. 18** requires traceability at all stages, implemented as **one step back / one step forward**: identify who supplied you and to whom you supplied, and produce it to the authority **on demand**. **Art. 18(4)** requires food on the market to be adequately labelled or identified to facilitate traceability. Status: LIKELY.

**What one-step-forward does NOT require: you do NOT have to trace to the final consumer.** A gym that buys 5 cases is your customer and must be recorded; the member who buys one can is not. **This makes D2C and gym-fridge consignment sales administratively trivial — which is exactly the batch-1 channel.**

### 8.1 Minimum record set

| Record | Content | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **INBOUND (one step back)** | Co-packer name and address, delivery date, product, **batch/lot number**, best-before date, quantity received, invoice/delivery-note reference | Art. 18 |
| **OUTBOUND (one step forward)** | Customer name and address, dispatch date, product, **batch/lot number dispatched**, quantity, invoice/delivery-note reference | Art. 18 |
| **BATCH LINKAGE** | The bridge: which inbound lot went to which customer. **The single most common failure point.** If you cannot say which gyms got lot L2604, you must recall **everything** | Makes a recall survivable |
| **LOT MARK ON THE CAN** | Every prepacked food must bear a lot/batch identification. **Where the durability date includes at least the day and month, that date can serve as the lot mark.** | Lot-marking rules (Directive 2011/91/EU, absorbed into FIC practice) — LIKELY, **verify** |
| **NON-CONFORMANCE / DISPOSAL LOG** | Damaged, expired or returned cans and their disposal | Prevents re-entry into the chain |

**Batch 1 is ONE lot of ~1,700 cans.** That is the best possible starting position: a single inbound line and a customer list you can hold on one page. **Get the discipline right at 1,700 cans and it scales; get it wrong and the first recall takes out the whole batch.**

### 8.2 Retention period

**UNKNOWN — NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION.** EU implementing guidance sets tiered retention keyed to shelf life; separately, **Hungarian accounting law requires retention of accounting documents for 8 years.**

### 9.2 Why a 330 ml can of protein soda is a normal food

**The decisive test is DOSE FORM.** Directive 2002/46/EC defines food supplements as concentrated sources of nutrients marketed **in dose form** — capsules, pastilles, tablets, pills, sachets of powder, ampoules of liquid, drop-dispensing bottles and other similar forms **designed to be taken in measured small quantities.**

- A **330 ml carbonated drink consumed as a drink** is not a measured small quantity in dose form. **→ Not a food supplement.**
- **Reg. 609/2013 contains no "sports food" category** — the Commission expressly declined to create one, leaving foods for sportspeople under general food law. **→ Not an FSMP.**
- **High protein content alone does not change classification.** Milk, drinking yoghurt and RTD protein shakes are sold across the EU as normal foods.

**→ CONCLUSION: normal food. NO pre-market product notification, NO OGYEI fee.**
**Status: LIKELY — legal reasoning, NOT VERIFIED 2026-08-16. This is load-bearing for the whole business plan and must be confirmed by a Hungarian food lawyer or by NEBIH before launch.** It is the *favourable* answer and it is consistent with how clear-whey RTD beverages are sold across the EU.

### 9.3 The four traps that could flip it — and ARCH F has one live

### 11.3 Applying it to ARCH F

- **WPI is a processed product of animal origin.** All commercial WPI comes from an EU-approved (or equivalence-listed) dairy establishment and carries an approval number on its CoA. **That satisfies the second half of Art. 1(2)** — provided you actually demand the number.
- **Hydrolysed collagen is also a processed product of animal origin**, governed by **Annex III Section XV (Collagen)** (Section XIV = Gelatine). Same logic, same requirement: **your collagen supplier must be an approved collagen establishment with an approval number on the CoA.**
- **Does the finished drink contain products of plant origin?** Yes on any normal reading: **natural lemon-lime flavouring of plant origin, citric acid, malic acid**, and — if the stevia option is taken — steviol glycosides. Water is not a plant product, but the flavour and acid system is.

**The genuine contrary reading you must neutralise:** **Annex I of Reg. 853/2004** defines "dairy products" as *processed products resulting from the processing of raw milk or from further processing of such processed products*. (EC) 999/2001.** For bovine collagen this governs specified risk material controls and permitted raw materials. **Collagen from HIDES AND SKINS is the low-risk route and is treated more permissively than bone-derived material.** STRUCTURAL — HIGH CONFIDENCE on 999/2001 being the instrument; `UNVERIFIED RECALL` on the hide-vs-bone detail. **→ Specify BOVINE HIDE-DERIVED collagen, and require a TSE/BSE compliance statement referencing Reg. 999/2001 plus the country of origin of the hides and its BSE risk categorisation.** ARCH F's spec already says hide-derived — **hold the supplier to it.**

**Whey protein isolate and hydrolysate are ordinary food ingredients, not additives and not novel foods.** No authorisation, no max level under 1333/2008. **Their only regulatory weight is the animal-origin analysis above.** STRUCTURAL — HIGH CONFIDENCE.

## 13. OFFICIAL CONTROLS AND FINES

### 13.1 What an inspection of a storage-and-distribution operator looks at

Derived from Annex II of Reg. 852/2004 and Arts. 18–19 of Reg. 178/2002. **LIKELY — offered as a preparation checklist.**

1. **Registration status** — are you registered, do you hold a FELIR azonosito, **does the registered activity match what you are actually doing**
2. **Traceability records** — the classic test is *"show me where lot X came from and where it went"*, answered **from your records, on the spot**
3. **The GHP/HACCP documentation** — does it exist, does it describe *this* operation, is it being followed, are the records filled in
4. **Premises** — cleanliness, pest evidence, segregation from non-food items, temperature, off-floor storage
5. **Stock condition** — expired product held with sellable stock, dented/damaged cans, FEFO rotation
6. **Labelling** — Hungarian language, mandatory particulars, **allergen: MILK**, nutrition declaration, lot mark, best-before, **name and address of the responsible FBO (Art. 8(1) — yours)**
7. **Claims** — that "magas feherjetartalmu" and "zero sugar" are substantiated and permitted
8. **Withdrawal/recall procedure** — does one exist in writing
9. **Supervision-fee declaration** — filed
10. **Co-packer documentation** — specifications, CoAs, shelf-life justification, allergen statement, establishment numbers

**THE THREE MOST LIKELY FINDINGS FOR THIS SPECIFIC BUSINESS, IN ORDER:**
**(1) traceability records that cannot link an inbound lot to an outbound customer; (2) a garage that co-stores food with chemicals or cannot hold the required temperature; (3) a label detail — most probably the Hungarian-language requirement or the emphasised milk allergen.**
**All three are preventable at zero cost.** Status: **ESTIMATE** — judgement, not a sourced statistic.
