### 2.1 Legibility and field of vision — Art. 12, 13

- **Art. 12(2):** mandatory particulars on the package or on a label attached to it. **A shrink sleeve is a label attached to the package — compliant.** `CORE-UNVERIFIED`
- **Art. 13(2):** x-height **≥ 1.2 mm** (x-height defined in Annex IV).
- **Art. 13(3):** if largest surface < 80 cm², x-height may drop to ≥ 0.9 mm.
- **Art. 16(2):** if largest surface < 10 cm², ingredient list and nutrition declaration may be omitted.

`ESTIMATE` — printable area of a standard 330 ml can body: diameter ≈ 66 mm, printable height ≈ 115 mm. Circumference = π × 66 = **207 mm**. Area = 207 × 115 = 23,805 mm² ≈ **238 cm²**.

> 238 cm² ≫ 80 cm² → **the 0.9 mm concession does NOT apply. Use x-height ≥ 1.2 mm on every mandatory particular.**
> 238 cm² ≫ 10 cm² → **no Art. 16(2) exemption. Full ingredient list and full nutrition declaration are mandatory.**

**Tell the designer this in the brief, not in a revision:** 1.2 mm x-height is *not* 6 pt. Measure the lowercase "x" of the chosen face and hit 1.2 mm — for most sans faces that is **7–8 pt**. A sleeve set at 5 pt "because it fits" is a non-compliant label.

- **Art. 13(5):** the **name of the food** and the **net quantity** must be in the **same field of vision** (Art. 2(2)(k): all surfaces readable from one viewing point ≈ the front ~⅓ of the can circumference).

> **Front panel must carry, together: legal name + `édesítőszerekkel` + `330 ml`.**

## 4. WHAT THE PRODUCT MAY LEGALLY BE CALLED

### 4.1 The Art. 17 hierarchy `CORE-UNVERIFIED`

1. **Legal name** — prescribed by EU or national law.
2. Failing that, **customary name** — accepted by consumers without further explanation.
3. Failing that, **descriptive name** — clear enough that consumers know the true nature of the food and can distinguish it from what it could be confused with.

**Art. 17(4): a brand name, trade mark or fancy name CANNOT substitute for the name of the food.** This is the single most common startup labelling failure. Your brand lockup in big letters on the front is a *fancy name*. It does not discharge Art. 9(1)(a). A separate, legible legal name must also be there.

### 4.2 Which tier applies

There is **no legal name** in EU or Hungarian law for a "protein soda", and **no customary name** — Hungarian consumers have no settled understanding of *fehérjeszóda*.

> **Tier 3 applies → a DESCRIPTIVE NAME is mandatory.** It must convey: (a) it is a drink, (b) it is carbonated, (c) it contains added protein, plus (d) the Annex III sweetener statement.

### 4.3 ⚠️ IS "SZÓDA" / "SZÓDAVÍZ" A PROTECTED TERM? — the direct answer

**`NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION`. Treat the following as a strong research lead, not a fact.**

- In Hungarian food law **`szikvíz`** is a *defined product*: potable water artificially carbonated with carbon dioxide, and nothing else. **`szódavíz`** is its everyday synonym and is understood to refer to the same defined product.
- Hungarian *szikvíz* also carries protected-heritage standing (recognised as a **hungarikum**), and an active trade association polices misuse of the term.
- The product definition is believed to sit in the **Magyar Élelmiszerkönyv (Codex Alimentarius Hungaricus)** as a mandatory directive on szikvíz. **The directive number is UNKNOWN and will not be guessed.**

**Two independent legal risks, and the second survives even if the first evaporates:**

| # | Risk | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Reserved designation.** Applying `szódavíz`/`szikvíz` to a flavoured, sweetened, protein-fortified drink breaches the reserved term, regardless of whether anyone is confused. | Magyar Élelmiszerkönyv, directive number unknown |
| 2 | **Misleading practice.** Calling a sweetened protein beverage a *szóda* invites the argument that consumers are misled as to the nature of the food. **This risk exists even if risk 1 does not.** | FIC **Art. 7(1)(a)** |

> ### VERDICT
> **Do NOT use `szóda`, `szódavíz` or `fehérje szóda` as the NAME OF THE FOOD.**
>
> The English word **"SODA"** carries no Hungarian protection — but it also carries no safe harbour, because Art. 7 applies to the overall impression **in any language**. Using "SODA" as a stylised brand device, with a compliant descriptive name printed separately and legibly in the same field of vision, is defensible. **Get that specific lockup signed off in the one paid label review.**
>
> **Note a direct conflict between the dossiers:** A5 §11 casually offers *"fehérje szódá"* as a legal substitute for the forbidden word *energiaital*. **A4 §3.3 is right and A5 is wrong on this point.** Do not take label copy from the A5 red-list substitute column.

### 4.4 Is `üdítőital` safe? `NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION`

`üdítőital` (soft drink) is itself believed to be described in the **Magyar Élelmiszerkönyv**, and certain soft-drink designations carry **minimum fruit-content requirements**. A protein drink with no fruit juice may not qualify for some *üdítőital* sub-designations. **Verify the MÉ soft-drink directive before using this word in the name.** Verify at `https://portal.nebih.gov.hu/` (Élelmiszerkönyv / MÉ irányelvek).

## 5. ALLERGENS — Art. 21 + Annex II

`CORE-UNVERIFIED`. **Highest-consequence section in this file. A missed milk allergen is a recall, and a recall ends this business.**

### 5.1 The Annex II entries that touch ARCH F

| Annex II entry | English | **Hungarian — WORKING TRANSLATION, VERIFY VERBATIM** |
|---|---|---|
| Milk | Milk and products thereof (including lactose) | `Tej és az abból készült termékek (beleértve a laktózt)` |
| Fish | Fish and products thereof | `Hal és a belőle készült termékek` |
| Soya | Soybeans and products thereof | `Szójabab és a belőle készült termékek` |

**Whey protein isolate → MILK. Always. No exceptions.** "Isolate", "lactose-free" and "<0.1 g lactose" do **not** remove the milk allergen declaration. The allergen is the milk **protein**, and whey protein *is* milk protein. Any supplier or designer who tells you otherwise is wrong.

**Hydrolysed BOVINE collagen → NOT an allergen** under Annex II. Beef is not an Annex II entry. `CORE-UNVERIFIED`

**If you ever switch to MARINE collagen → FISH becomes a mandatory emphasised allergen.** The narrow Annex II exemptions for fish gelatine (vitamin/carotenoid carrier; fining agent in beer and wine) do **not** cover hydrolysed fish collagen used as a protein ingredient. **ARCH F is bovine — this does not bite. Do not let a cheaper marine quote change the formula without changing the sleeve.**

**Soya:** watch soy lecithin as a carrier in the flavour system. **Get an allergen declaration on the specification sheet from every supplier — flavour house, WPI, collagen, leucine, tryptophan.** Free, and it is your Art. 6 substantiation file.

### 5.2 Emphasis — Art. 21(1)(a) — THE EXACT HUNGARIAN WORDING

Allergenic substances must be **emphasised through a typeset that clearly distinguishes them from the rest of the list of ingredients** — by **font, style or background colour**.

**What goes on your sleeve:**

> `Összetevők: víz, hidrolizált marhakollagén, **tejsavófehérje**-izolátum (**tej**), …`
> — with `tejsavófehérje` and `tej` set in **BOLD**.

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Which word is emphasised | The word that *refers to* the Annex II substance. In `tejsavófehérje-izolátum` the allergenic reference is the `tej` root. **Emphasising the whole compound `tejsavófehérje` is the conventional and clean solution.** |
| Method | **Bold.** Market standard, safest. ALL-CAPS also works but reads badly at 1.2 mm. |
| Do NOT | Rely on **colour alone** — fails in mono print and for colour-blind consumers. |
| Belt-and-braces | `**tejsavófehérje**-izolátum (**tej**)` — recommended for a fitness product where the buyer scans fast. |

### 5.3 No separate "Contains:" statement needed — Art. 21(1)(b)

Where an ingredient list is present and the allergen is emphasised inside it, a separate "contains…" statement is **NOT required**. A voluntary `Allergéninformáció: tejet tartalmaz.` may be added and is good practice for a fitness channel, but it must not contradict the list.

### 5.4 "May contain" — voluntary, and not a free shield

`Nyomokban … tartalmazhat` is **not regulated by FIC** and is **voluntary**. It may only be used where a documented risk assessment justifies it. Over-use is itself potentially misleading under Art. 7. **Action: get the co-packer's line-allergen matrix from Opre Label / Bubee in writing before artwork.** If their line also runs nut or milkshake products you may need it.

## 6. ⭐ THE ANNEX VI PART A QUESTION — added proteins of animal origin

**This is the item that is easiest to miss and it changes the PRODUCT NAME if it applies. It does not apply. Here is the full analysis so you can hand it to a lawyer.**

### 6.1 What Annex VI Part A actually contains

Annex VI is headed **"Name of the food and specific accompanying particulars"**. **Part A — "Particulars accompanying the name of the food"** contains a numbered list. Two points are candidates here:

| Point | Substance of the rule | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Point 4** | Where a **component or ingredient that consumers expect to be normally used or naturally present has been substituted** with a different component or ingredient, the labelling must bear — in addition to the ingredient list — a clear indication of the substituting component, **in close proximity to the name of the food** and at a specified minimum character size. | **ALL FOODS.** No scope limit. |
| **Point 5** | In the case of **meat products, meat preparations and fishery products** containing **added proteins as such, including hydrolysed proteins, of a different animal origin**, the name of the food must indicate the presence of those proteins **and their origin**. | **Meat products, meat preparations, fishery products ONLY.** |

`CORE-UNVERIFIED` on the structure. **⚠️ The brief calls the added-animal-protein rule "point 4"; the numbering above places it at point 5, with point 4 being the substituted-ingredient rule. The point NUMBER could not be verified. Do not cite a point number to anyone until it is read off the consolidated text. The load-bearing element — the scope limitation to meat/meat-preparation/fishery products — is held with high confidence.**

### 6.2 Does point 5 (added animal proteins) apply to ARCH F?

ARCH F contains **hydrolysed bovine collagen — a hydrolysed protein of animal origin — added as such.** That is exactly the substance the rule is about. The only question is scope.

> **A carbonated flavoured beverage is not a meat product, not a meat preparation, and not a fishery product.**
>
> ### VERDICT: point 5 DOES NOT APPLY. The NAME OF THE FOOD does NOT have to say `marhakollagénnel` or `marhafehérjével`.

If it *had* applied, the legal name would have had to become something like `Szénsavas, fehérjetartalmú ital marhakollagénnel, édesítőszerekkel` — a materially worse name for a fitness product and a front-panel space problem. **You dodge this. Confirm the scope wording in the review anyway; it is one line of reading.**

### 6.3 Does point 4 (substitution) apply? — the subtler risk

Point 4 has **no scope limit**, so it must be reasoned through, not waved away.

**The argument that it applies:** a consumer buying a 20 g "protein" fitness drink arguably expects **complete protein** (whey/milk/soy). ARCH F is **50 % hydrolysed bovine collagen** — an incomplete protein, low in leucine and tryptophan. Someone could argue collagen "substitutes" the whey the consumer expected, forcing a `kollagénnel` indication in close proximity to the name at prescribed character size.

**The argument that it does not apply, which is the better one:**

1. Point 4 requires a component **"consumers expect to be normally used or naturally present"**. Protein soda is a **new category with no established consumer expectation** as to protein source. There is no norm to depart from.
2. Point 4 is aimed at like-for-like swaps in established foods (vegetable fat for milk fat in "cheese", plant protein for meat in a "ham"). A novel beverage has no predecessor composition.
3. Nothing is substituted — collagen is **added alongside** whey isolate, not in place of it. Both are declared.

> ### VERDICT: point 4 does not apply. `NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION` — this is the one naming question in this file where a competent lawyer could reasonably disagree with me, so put it on the review list explicitly.

### 6.4 What the collagen split DOES force on you — Art. 7(1)(a)

Independent of Annex VI, **FIC Art. 7(1)(a)** prohibits information misleading as to the **composition** of the food. A "20 g protein" front panel where half the protein is collagen is exactly the fact pattern an enforcement officer or a competitor would probe.

**The transparency that closes it, and it costs nothing:**

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Declare **`hidrolizált marhakollagén`** by its **specific name and species** in the ingredient list, in true descending weight order (it will sit near the top). | Declare it as `kollagén peptidek` or `kollagén` with no species. |
| Let the ingredient list do the work. | Add a big front-panel `KOLLAGÉNNEL` flash in batch 1 — that triggers **QUID** (§8) and forces you to print **3,0 %**, which reads badly next to "10 g". |
| Keep the whey-plus-leucine story for off-pack marketing. | Imply the protein is whey-only. |

**Four reasons the species is declared even though no rule in Annex VI forces it:**
1. **Art. 18(2)** — an ingredient takes its **specific name**; "kollagén" alone hides a material fact.
2. **Allergen law** — if the collagen ever changes to marine, `hal` becomes mandatory and emphasised.
3. **Religious/dietary** — porcine collagen undeclared is a serious trust and misleading-practice exposure in a gym channel. Bovine must be visibly bovine so nobody assumes porcine.
4. **Channel expectation** — halal, kosher and vegetarian buyers read this list.

**BSE/TSE:** collagen from bovine hides is understood to be permitted raw material under Reg. (EC) No 999/2001 with **no mandatory consumer-facing on-pack statement**; controls sit upstream with the collagen manufacturer. `NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION`. **Do not rely on this — get the supplier's written TSE/BSE statement in the specification pack.** It is free and you will be asked for it by any serious retailer later.

## 7. INGREDIENT LIST — Art. 18 + Annex VII

`CORE-UNVERIFIED`.

### 7.1 Heading and order

- **Art. 18(1):** headed by or preceded by **`Összetevők:`**
- **Art. 18(2):** descending order of weight **as recorded at the time of use** in manufacture.

### 7.2 Water — Annex VII Part A

Added water is listed in order of its weight in the **finished** product, calculated by subtracting the weight of all other ingredients from the finished weight. The <5 % exemption is irrelevant here.

> **Water is ~90 %+ of a 330 ml can. It is by far the largest ingredient. It MUST be declared and it MUST be FIRST: `Víz`.**

> If carbonated water is the input, `Szénsavas víz` may be used instead. `Víz` + a separate `szén-dioxid (E 290)` entry is the more common construction for a canned carbonated soft drink and is what is drafted below.

## 7B. NUTRITION DECLARATION — Art. 29–35

### 7B.1 Mandatory content and ORDER — Art. 30(1) + Annex XV

The declaration comprises, **in this exact order** — a designer who reorders it to put protein first creates a non-compliant label:

1. **Energia** — in **both kJ and kcal**, **kJ first**
2. **Zsír**
3. **— amelyből telített zsírsavak**
4. **Szénhidrát**
5. **— amelyből cukrok**
6. **Fehérje**
7. **Só**

Protein may be repeated **outside** the table as a claim. The table order stands.

### 7B.2 Basis of expression — Art. 32, 33

- **Art. 32(2):** mandatory basis is **per 100 g or per 100 ml**. For a liquid: **per 100 ml**. This column is never optional.
- **Art. 33:** **in addition** (never instead), the declaration may be given **per portion / per consumption unit** → **`1 doboz (330 ml)`**.

> **USE TWO COLUMNS: `100 ml` and `1 doboz (330 ml)`.** The per-can column is where **20 g fehérje** lands, which is the entire commercial proposition. 6,1 g reads like nothing; 20 g sells the can.

### 7B.3 Energy conversion factors — Annex XIV `CORE-UNVERIFIED`

Energy is **calculated with these factors**, not by converting kcal→kJ with 4.184.

| Nutrient | kJ/g | kcal/g |
|---|---|---|
| Protein | 17 | 4 |
| Carbohydrate (excl. polyols) | 17 | 4 |
| Fat | 37 | 9 |
| Polyols | 10 | 2.4 |
| **Erythritol** | **0** | **0** |
| Fibre | 8 | 2 |
| Organic acid | 13 | 3 |

### 7B.4 The ARCH F numbers `DERIVED`

**Protein per 100 ml:** 20 ÷ 3.30 = **6.06 g/100 ml** → declare **`6,1 g`**

**Energy from protein per can:** 20 × 17 = **340 kJ**; 20 × 4 = **80 kcal**
**Per 100 ml:** 340 ÷ 3.30 = **103 kJ**; 80 ÷ 3.30 = **24.2 kcal**

> **80 kcal per can is the arithmetic FLOOR at 20 g protein.** Nothing below it is possible. **Tell whoever writes marketing copy: "under 80 calories" cannot be said.**

**⚠️ ARCH F specific — the free amino acids raise the DECLARED protein figure.** Protein is legally defined as **total Kjeldahl nitrogen × 6.25** (FIC Annex I). Free amino acids contain nitrogen and are captured by that method.

```
L-leucine  C6H13NO2,  MW 131.17, 1 N (14.007) → N fraction 10.68 %
   1.7 g × 0.1068 = 0.1815 g N × 6.25 = 1.13 g protein-equivalent
L-tryptophan C11H12N2O2, MW 204.23, 2 N (28.014) → N fraction 13.72 %
   0.15 g × 0.1372 = 0.0206 g N × 6.25 = 0.13 g protein-equivalent
                                          TOTAL ≈ +1.26 g
```
`DERIVED` — **so a can built to deliver 20 g of protein from WPI + collagen will likely ANALYSE at ~21.3 g by Kjeldahl.**

> **Two consequences, both good:**
> 1. Declaring **20 g** gives you **~1.3 g of built-in overage** against the tolerance check. Protein under-delivery is the one number a competitor will pay a lab to test. **Declare 20 g, deliver 21+.**
> 2. Energy rises correspondingly: 21.3 × 4 = **85 kcal/can ≈ 26 kcal/100 ml**. Still far below every claim ceiling (§16).

**Final declared values must come from lab analysis of an actual production batch, not from the supplier's theoretical calculation.** Analysis cost: **UNKNOWN — QUOTE REQUIRED** (cross-reference the labs workstream).

### 7B.5 Rounding `NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION` on the exact table

**Rounding rules are NOT in the Regulation.** They sit in the Commission's *"Guidance document for competent authorities for the control of compliance with EU legislation … with regard to the setting of tolerances for nutrient values declared on a label"* (December 2012). Verify at `https://food.ec.europa.eu/` — search "tolerances guidance nutrient values".

Recalled principles, **treat as a lead**:

| Nutrient | Rounding |
|---|---|
| Energy | nearest **1 kJ** and **1 kcal** |
| Fat, carbohydrate, sugars, protein, fibre | nearest **0.1 g** if <10 g; nearest **1 g** if ≥10 g |
| Salt | nearest **0.01 g** if <1 g; nearest **0.1 g** if ≥1 g |
| Negligible values | may be declared `0 g` or `<0,5 g` |

### 8B.2 The polyol laxative threshold — does it trigger?

**Threshold: more than 10 % added polyols.** `DERIVED`:

```
10 % of a 330 ml (≈330 g) can = 33 g of polyol per can
Realistic erythritol use in a carbonated soft drink: 1–3 g / 100 ml
   → 3.3–10 g per can → 1–3 % of the can
3 % ≪ 10 %  →  WARNING NOT TRIGGERED
```

> **The `túlzott fogyasztása hashajtó hatású lehet` warning would only appear above ~33 g of polyol per can** — undrinkably sweet and osmotically aggressive. **Not a practical concern.**

**But sidestep polyols entirely anyway.** A4 §7.3 flags **erythritol (E 968) legality in beverages (food category 14.1.4) as UNRESOLVED and formulation-blocking** — its authorisation is category-specific with maximum levels, and it is believed to have been restricted or excluded in beverages historically, with a possible post-2023 amendment whose instrument number is `UNKNOWN`.

> **RECOMMENDATION — build the sweetening on high-intensity sweeteners only:**
> **sucralose (E 955)** + **acesulfame K (E 950)**, or **steviol glycosides (E 960a)** if a "natural" story is wanted.
>
> Zero bulk, zero energy, cheaper per can than erythritol, and it removes the polyol question, the laxative-warning question and the erythritol-legality question in one move. Recalled maximum levels in FC 14.1.4 (E 950 ≈ 350 mg/l, E 955 ≈ 300 mg/l, E 960 ≈ 80 mg/l steviol equivalents) are **`NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION` and must NOT be used for formulation.**
>
> **Make the flavour house warrant additive compliance in writing.** It is free and it transfers the risk off you.

## 10. LOT MARKING — Directive 2011/91/EU

`CORE-UNVERIFIED` on the Directive; `NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION` on the Hungarian implementing instrument (verify at `https://njt.hu/`).

- Foodstuffs must bear an indication identifying the **batch/lot**.
- The indication must be **preceded by the letter "L"** — **except** where it is clearly distinguishable from the other label indications.
- **Exemption:** a lot code is **not required** where the **date of minimum durability appears with at least the uncoded DAY and MONTH**.

> ### ⚠️ THE TRAP
> A 9–18 month shelf life lets you print **month + year only** (`Minőségét megőrzi: 2027.09. végéig`). But that date does **not** include the day → **the exemption fails → an "L…" lot code becomes MANDATORY.**
>
> Print a **full day-month-year date** and the exemption applies.

> ### **RECOMMENDATION: print BOTH a full `ÉÉÉÉ.HH.NN.` date AND an `L…` lot code.**
> 1. Unambiguously compliant on either reading.
> 2. A lot code is **essential** for traceability under **Reg. (EC) No 178/2002 Art. 18** and for any withdrawal or recall — which you must be able to execute regardless of labelling law. With 1,700 cans across gym fridges, "which cans do I pull?" is a question you will not be able to answer without one.
> 3. **It costs nothing.** Both are applied by the same inkjet coder at the filling line.

**Both are applied by the CO-PACKER's coder, not pre-printed on the sleeve.** Ask Opre Label / Bubee: coder format, character height, and where on the can body or base it lands. **Leave a clear coding window in the sleeve artwork.** A code printed over a busy graphic is unreadable, and an unreadable lot code is a failed recall.

### 12.2 ⚠️ COUNTRY OF ORIGIN — Art. 26 — the "magyar termék" trap

`CORE-UNVERIFIED` on Art. 26 and Reg. (EU) 2018/775.

Origin is **not generally mandatory** for a soft drink. It becomes mandatory where:
- **Art. 26(2)(a)** — failure to indicate might mislead as to the true country of origin; or
- **Art. 26(3) + Reg. (EU) 2018/775** — the **origin of the food is given** (in words **or by implication: flags, national colours, national landmarks, a map**) and it differs from the origin of the **primary ingredient**. Then either the primary ingredient's origin must be given, or a statement that it is not the same: `A [primary ingredient] nem Magyarországról származik.`

## 13. NET QUANTITY AND THE ℮ MARK

### 13.1 Net quantity — Art. 23 + Annex IX `CORE-UNVERIFIED`

Liquids are expressed in **volume** → **`330 ml`**. Must be in the **same field of vision as the name of the food** (Art. 13(5)).

**Minimum character height for the nominal quantity is set by prepackaging METROLOGY law (Directive 76/211/EEC as amended), not by FIC — and it is LARGER than the FIC 1.2 mm x-height.** Recalled scale, `NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION`:

| Nominal quantity | Min. height |
|---|---|
| ≤ 50 ml | 2 mm |
| > 50 to 200 ml | 3 mm |
| **> 200 to 1000 ml** | **4 mm** |
| > 1000 ml | 6 mm |

> **330 ml → minimum 4 mm character height.** This is a *height* requirement, not an x-height requirement, and it is bigger than the ingredient-list minimum. **Designers routinely get this wrong. Verify the table before artwork sign-off.**

### 13.2 Is 330 ml an allowed nominal size? — YES

`CORE-UNVERIFIED`. **Directive 2007/45/EC deregulated nominal quantities**, abolishing mandatory fixed-size ranges for most prepacked products and retaining prescribed ranges only for a short list (notably wines and spirit drinks).

> **330 ml is a free nominal size. There is no legal obstacle.** The constraint on can size is **purely industrial** — 330 ml standard and sleek are the formats for which bodies, ends and filling lines are universally available. **Stay at 330 ml for supply-chain reasons, not legal ones.**

### 13.3 The ℮ mark — Directive 76/211/EEC — VOLUNTARY, AND NOT YOURS TO PRINT

`CORE-UNVERIFIED` on the principle.

The **℮** (estimated sign) is **entirely voluntary**. It is a declaration that the prepackage was filled under the EU **average-quantity system**, and it guarantees free circulation across the single market.

**It may NOT simply be printed.** Using ℮ requires that the **PACKER — your co-packer, not you**:
- operates a statistical filling-control system meeting the average-quantity rules (mean ≥ nominal; defined tolerable negative errors; no package below twice the tolerable error);
- keeps records available to the metrological authority;
- is subject to national metrology supervision. **Competent Hungarian body: `UNKNOWN — NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION`.**

Formal requirements: **≥ 3 mm high**, in the **same field of vision as the nominal quantity**.

> **ACTION: put one line in the Opre Label / Bubee RFQ — "Do you operate an ℮-mark-compliant average-quantity filling control system, and may we print ℮?"**
> If yes, you get it free and it helps a later Austrian move. **If no, omit it — it is not required for domestic Hungarian sale. Printing ℮ without the system is a false declaration.**

### 16.1 ⭐ HIGH PROTEIN — the arithmetic, and why it is unbreakable

**The condition** (Annex to Reg. 1924/2006) `NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION` on verbatim text:

> **SOURCE OF PROTEIN** — may only be made where **at least 12 % of the energy value of the food is provided by protein**.
> **HIGH PROTEIN** — may only be made where **at least 20 % of the energy value of the food is provided by protein**.

**The structural reason you win:** both conditions are **ratios of energy**, not absolute amounts and **not per 100 ml**. Serving size is irrelevant. **A very low-calorie, high-protein drink is the single most favourable product geometry that exists for this claim** — every calorie removed from non-protein sources *increases* the protein energy share.

**Energy factor** (FIC Annex XIV): protein = **17 kJ/g = 4 kcal/g**.

**THE ARITHMETIC** `DERIVED`:

```
STEP 1 — energy from protein
   20 g × 4 kcal/g = 80 kcal from protein

STEP 2 — protein's share of total energy, P = 80 / E
   E =  80 kcal  →  P = 80/80  = 100.0 %
   E =  85 kcal  →  P = 80/85  =  94.1 %   ← ARCH F with the amino-acid nitrogen (§7B.4)
   E =  90 kcal  →  P = 80/90  =  88.9 %
   E = 100 kcal  →  P = 80/100 =  80.0 %

STEP 3 — test
   HIGH PROTEIN needs ≥ 20 %.  We have 80–100 %.  PASS by 4.0–5.0×
   SOURCE OF    needs ≥ 12 %.  We have 80–100 %.  PASS by 6.7–8.3×

STEP 4 — the CEILING: at what total energy does HIGH PROTEIN fail?
   80 / E ≥ 0.20  →  E ≤ 80 / 0.20 = 400 kcal per can
   (SOURCE OF ceiling: 80 / 0.12 = 666.7 kcal per can)
```

> ## **HIGH PROTEIN holds up to 400 kcal per can at 20 g protein. You are at 80–100 kcal — 20–25 % of the ceiling.**
> **To break the claim you would have to add ~300 kcal of non-protein material to the can.** Batch variation, Kjeldahl analytical uncertainty and later flavour-system changes cannot plausibly touch it. **This claim is structurally safe.**

**THE FLOOR — and this is a cost lever the founder should know about** `DERIVED`:

```
At a protein level of P grams with X kcal of NON-protein energy in the can:
   share = 4P / (4P + X) ≥ 0.20  →  3.2P ≥ 0.2X  →  P ≥ X / 16

ARCH F has essentially zero non-protein energy. Even assuming a generous
X = 20 kcal of non-protein energy:
   P ≥ 20 / 16 = 1.25 g protein

Checking the brief's 12 g question directly:
   12 g protein × 4 = 48 kcal from protein
   claim fails only if 48 / E < 0.20, i.e. E > 240 kcal per can
   ARCH F at 12 g protein would land at ~48–60 kcal per can  →  PASS
```

> ## **HIGH PROTEIN qualifies at EVERY protein level down to 12 g — and far below it, down to roughly 1.3 g.**
>
> ### What this means for the money: **the 20 g is a MARKETING decision, not a legal one.**
> The claim does not force 20 g. Consumer expectation and the competitive shelf force 20 g. **If ingredient cost ever becomes the binding constraint on unit economics — and at 213 HUF/can pilot ingredients ≈ 0.59 EUR it is the largest single line — you can reformulate downward and keep `MAGAS FEHÉRJETARTALMÚ` intact.** `DERIVED (213 ÷ 363 = 0.587 EUR)`. **Do not reformulate for batch 1** — "20 g" is the number the gym channel reads. But know the lever exists for batch 2.

**Protein is legally measured as total Kjeldahl nitrogen × 6.25** (FIC Annex I). **There is NO protein-quality requirement in the nutrition-claim Annex** — PDCAAS/DIAAS are irrelevant to the claim. Collagen counts. (See §16.6 for the residual risk this creates.)

### 16.2 ⛔ LOW CALORIE — why it is impossible forever

`DERIVED`:

```
LOW ENERGY limit for a LIQUID: 20 kcal / 100 ml
Maximum permitted energy in a 330 ml can: 20 × 3.30 = 66 kcal per can

Energy from ARCH F's protein ALONE: 20 g × 4 = 80 kcal
80 kcal > 66 kcal  →  FAIL, before a single other ingredient is added

Maximum protein compatible with LOW ENERGY on a 330 ml can:
   66 ÷ 4 = 16.5 g — and only if the can contained literally nothing
   else contributing any energy.

Actual figures per 100 ml: 80 ÷ 3.30 = 24.2 kcal;  100 ÷ 3.30 = 30.3 kcal
Both > 20 kcal/100 ml.
```

> ### **This is not a formulation problem you can solve. It is arithmetic.**
> **This is the single easiest and most expensive mistake for a founder thinking "only 80 calories!"** — 80 kcal per *can* is not low-energy per *100 ml*, and 100 ml is the basis the law uses for beverages.
>
> **What you CAN print:** the bare number **`80 kcal`**. A factual statement of a nutrition-declaration value is not a nutrition claim.
> **What converts it into an illegal claim:** any qualitative or comparative adjective — `csak`, `mindössze`, `only`, `just`, `alacsony`, `light`, `könnyű`. `NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION` on where exactly Hungarian enforcement draws the line on `csak`/`mindössze`. **Safe default: print the bare number with NO adjective.**

### 16.3 CAFFEINE CLAIMS — the position

`NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION` on the refusing instrument.

They were **not included in the Union list**.

> **Net effect: there is NO authorised health claim for caffeine in the EU. Every phrase a caffeinated product wants — energy, focus, alertness, endurance, performance, "pre-workout" — is unavailable via caffeine.**

> The exact Commission Regulation number refusing them is **`UNKNOWN` and will not be guessed.** Verify in the EU Register, filter: caffeine, status non-authorised.

### 16.6 ⚠️ THE ONE RESIDUAL CLAIMS RISK IN ARCH F — collagen + the muscle-mass claim

> **DOSSIER CONFLICT WITH THE AGREED PLAN.** A5 §8.4 warns that using the **muscle-mass health claims (#11, #12) on a product whose protein is wholly or largely collagen** is an area of known national-authority scrutiny — collagen is an incomplete protein low in leucine and tryptophan, and its muscle-mass effect is contested. **A5's recommended mitigation is "use whey isolate as the primary protein and collagen only as a secondary addition, or omit collagen from SKU 1."**

> **ARCH F is 50 % collagen by protein (10 g collagen + 10 g whey isolate).**

**The defence, which is strong and is exactly why ARCH F is built the way it is:**

1. **The legal condition is purely QUANTITATIVE.** The 432/2012 condition of use is "at least a source of protein as referred to in the SOURCE OF PROTEIN claim". You are at 80–100 % of energy from protein against 12 %. **The condition is met on its face.** There is no protein-quality gate in the text.
2. **ARCH F is not collagen-dominant.** It is a **50/50 blend with whey protein isolate** — a complete protein — as half the load.
3. **ARCH F explicitly fixes the two amino acids collagen lacks:** **1.7 g L-leucine** and **150 mg L-tryptophan** were added for precisely this reason. That is a documented formulation answer to the scrutiny, not an accident.
