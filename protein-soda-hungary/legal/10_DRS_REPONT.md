## 2. ⚠️ THE "IT'S A MILK PRODUCT SO IT'S EXEMPT" ARGUMENT — WRONG, AND DANGEROUS

**Someone will make this argument to you.** A co-packer, a designer, an adviser, or your own optimism. It goes: *the drink contains whey protein, whey is dairy, milk and milk-based beverages are excluded from DRS, therefore we are exempt.*

**It is very likely wrong. If you print 20,000 sleeves on the strength of it, the cans are unsellable above the de minimis ceiling and the money is gone.**

### 2.1 What was actually found

| Finding | Status |
|---|---|
| *"Milk and milk-based beverages are excluded from mandatory DRS"* | Dossier A2 tagged this **CONFIRMED**. **Dossier A6 §4.1 downgrades it to LIKELY** — A2 could not open the decree and captured it from guidance snippets. **The exact exclusion wording and the exact customs codes were never obtained.** |
| The verbatim product-category list / annex (*melléklet*) of 450/2023 | **UNKNOWN — could not be read.** This is the gap that would settle it. |

### 2.2 Three independent reasons it does not rescue your product

**(1) The exclusion is drawn by customs code on *milk*, not by "contains milk protein."** Every European milk carve-out — and the derogation in the EU Single-Use Plastics Directive (Directive (EU) 2019/904) these national carve-outs descend from — is written around **milk and milk products** by CN/KN headings **0401 and 0402** and the milk-products list in Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013. Those headings cover **milk and cream**. They do not cover a water-based flavoured carbonated drink containing whey protein isolate as a functional ingredient. **Status: reasoning shown so it can be checked, not relied on.**

**(2) Your own product definition defeats it.** The agreed product is a **clear, carbonated, soda experience — explicitly not a milkshake**. A clear carbonated flavoured beverage classifies as **CN 2202** (waters, including flavoured, and other non-alcoholic beverages) — the archetypal in-scope DRS category. **The very positioning that makes the product commercially interesting is the positioning that puts it inside DRS. You cannot have the clear-soda brand and the dairy-drink exemption.**

**(3) The exclusion exists for a hygiene reason, not a protein reason.** Milk carve-outs exist because milk residue in a reverse vending machine is a spoilage and hygiene problem. A clear, acidic (~pH 3.2), pasteurised soda presents none of that and is exactly what an RVM is built to accept.

### 2.3 The reverse trap — do not over-correct either

> **For ALLERGEN labelling, whey protein isolate is unambiguously MILK and must be declared and emphasised.** Dossier A4 §5.1: *"Whey protein isolate → MILK. Always. Non-negotiable."*
>
> **A product can simultaneously be a milk allergen for FIC purposes and a non-milk soft drink for DRS purposes.** Two different legal tests, two different answers. **Do not conflate them, and do not let anyone use the allergen declaration as evidence of DRS exemption.**

### 2.4 The one purchase that settles three questions at once

The same milk / non-milk classification question also drives:
- **DRS scope** (this file),
- the **ÁFA rate** — 27% standard vs 18% for certain milk-based products by VTSZ heading (see `07_HUNGARY_BUSINESS_SETUP.md` §5.1),
- **NETA** scope.

> **A single written classification opinion from a Hungarian adviser, on the final ARCH F recipe, settles DRS scope, the ÁFA rate and NETA in one go. Buy that opinion once. It is the highest-leverage professional spend in the project.**
> The instrument, if the adviser thinks there is a case, is a NAV **kötelező érvényű vámtarifa-besorolás** (binding tariff classification) once the recipe and VTSZ are fixed.

### 6.2 The artwork risk that is worth real money

**Hungarian retail convention (the Aldi listing) shows the deposit as a separate line beside the *price*, not necessarily as a printed figure on the *pack*.**

- **If the decree requires only the MARK on the pack** and leaves the amount to the price display → your artwork survives a future deposit change.
- **If the AMOUNT (50 Ft) must be printed on the pack** → a deposit change strands your entire stock.

> **Ask MOHU this explicitly (§14, question 7). It is the difference between artwork that survives a rule change and artwork that does not.**

### 6.3 The irreversibility, stated plainly

> **The DRS mark and the DRS-registered GTIN are printed into the shrink sleeve at production. There is no retrofit.**
>
> Your packaging plan is **blank silver can + shrink sleeve** precisely because printed cans need 150,000–250,000 units (Rhodius published minimum: 250,000). **The sleeve is your only printable surface, and it is printed once, in a run, before filling.**
>
> **This means the DRS decision is taken ONCE, at the very start, and it binds either the ~5,000-unit ceiling or the full DRS regime onto the entire sleeve run.**
>
> **DECIDE BEFORE THE SLEEVE ARTWORK IS SENT TO PRINT. It cannot be deferred and it cannot be undone.**

### 6.4 Two instructions for whoever designs the sleeve — give them in the first briefing

The printable body area of a 330 ml can is **≈238 cm²** (π × 66 mm × 115 mm ≈ 23,805 mm², ESTIMATE from A4 §2.1). Because 238 cm² ≫ 80 cm², **no small-pack typography concession applies: every mandatory particular needs x-height ≥ 1.2 mm**, and the full ingredients list and full nutrition declaration are mandatory. Competing for that surface: legal name + *édesítőszerrel* + *330 ml* in one field of vision; ingredients with ***tejsavófehérje*** emphasised; the nutrition table; lot code; net quantity; FBO name and address.

1. **Even though batch 1 carries NO DRS mark, reserve a defined zone for it from layout v1.** Retrofitting means re-originating artwork, which is a real and non-trivial cost. **A layout designed with the zone present costs nothing extra today and makes the >5,000 version a re-export instead of a redesign.**
2. **Reserve the barcode zone to RVM standards, not shelf-scanner standards** — see §7.2. RVM reading is the harder constraint and it drives placement, curvature allowance and quiet-zone width. **This also costs nothing today.**

### 7.2 ⚠️ Are reseller / third-party barcodes acceptable?

**Honest answer: the dossiers do not address this, so — UNKNOWN — NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION.** But the question matters to you, because "buy a single barcode for EUR 15" resellers are the obvious way to dodge an unknown GS1 Hungary membership fee, and the reasoning points one way:

## 9. THE EPR CARVE-OUT — CROSSING THE THRESHOLD MAKES THE CAN CHEAPER

**This is the counter-intuitive finding, and it is worth understanding because it kills the "DRS is expensive" framing.**

> ***"A visszaváltási díjjal érintett csomagolások mentesülnek az EPR-díj alól."*** — **DRS-registered packaging is EXEMPT from the EPR fee.** Status: **LIKELY** (A2 §13.3 tagged this CONFIRMED).

**So crossing the threshold REMOVES the ~2.42–2.79 HUF/can EPR charge on the can itself.**

### 9.1 But EPR on shipper cartons and film NEVER goes away

**Secondary packaging is never DRS-exempt.** Your shipper cartons, shrink film and trays stay in EPR forever, at the **paper/cardboard rate P01 = 173 HUF/kg** (2026, per 33/2025. (XI. 28.) EM rendelet, SEARCH-VERIFIED).

**DERIVED:** a ~350 g shipper carton holding 24 cans → 0.35 kg × 173 HUF/kg = **60.55 HUF per case ÷ 24 = 2.52 HUF per can (EUR 0.0069)**. **ESTIMATE — the carton weight is an assumption; get the real weight from Opre Label / Bubee.**

**Consequence: you never escape OKIRkapu.** Even in the full DRS regime you keep an **EPR registration and a quarterly filing** for cartons and film. Registration prerequisites: Ügyfélkapu/DÁP, *meghatalmazás*, a **KÜJ** number, then the **KG: KGYF-NY** data package via OKIRkapu; quarterly return **by the 20th of the month after the quarter**; OKIR forwards to MOHU by the 25th; **MOHU invoices, payable within 15 days**.
