# 26b — HOW TO BUILD THE PROSPECT LIST YOURSELF (2–3 hours, €0)

**Read this before you complain that `26_GYM_PROSPECTS.csv` only has 11 rows.**

---

## Why the list is short, and why that is the right outcome

The research environment for this project could not open web pages — every gym website, directory and business listing returned `403` at the network egress proxy. Only search-result snippets were available.

The researcher was instructed never to invent a contact detail, and it didn't. Its own words:

> *"Writing them from memory would produce a confident-sounding strategy document with no evidential base, which is more dangerous than writing nothing, because the founder would plan channel strategy and pricing against it."*

**That is correct.** A CSV of 160 plausible-looking gyms with invented emails would have wasted weeks of your time and destroyed your credibility the first time you emailed a dead address.

**You can build a better list than any automated method, in one afternoon, for free.** Here is exactly how.

---

## The method, in priority order

### 1. Chain "Klubjaink" pages — highest yield per click
Hungarian gym chains publish a locations page listing **every site with address, phone, and often a club-specific email**. One page can produce 10–20 verified rows.

Start with: **Life1** (`life1.hu`) — already flagged priority 10 because it runs in-club Protein Bar retail.

### 2. AllYouCanMove and MultiSport partner directories ⭐ the single densest source
These publish their partner venues nationwide. Two reasons this list is better than a Google search:

- It is **comprehensive** — the venues want to be found
- Membership signals a venue that **already handles third-party commercial relationships**, which is exactly the behaviour you need

**Work this directory before anything else.**

### 3. CrossFit official affiliate map (`map.crossfit.com`)
Authoritative, complete, and CrossFit boxes are ideal first customers: strength-focused members, strong community, usually already sell drinks, and **the owner decides on the spot** rather than escalating to a head office.

### 4. Google Maps, for independents with no website
Search `konditerem`, `edzőterem`, `fitness`, `erőterem` by district. **Only trust a phone number if the listing is owner-verified.**

### 5. `Kapcsolat` / `Impresszum` pages
Hungarian websites are legally required to publish company identification. This usually yields a real HQ email **and** a company registration number — useful for confirming which legal entity actually operates a franchise.

### 6. Facebook / Instagram business pages
Often the only live channel for CrossFit boxes and martial arts gyms. The "About" block carries a published email surprisingly often.

### 7. University sport centres — high value, usually overlooked
TF (Testnevelési Egyetem) first, then BME, ELTE, Corvinus, Semmelweis, Szegedi Tudományegyetem, Debreceni Egyetem. Student-dense, credibility halo, and they run canteen and vending tenders.

### 8. Supplement stores — highest conversion rate of any category
They already buy, stock and resell exactly this product class. They understand wholesale terms without explanation.

---

## The one rule that keeps the list useful

> **Write a field only if you saw it on the page recorded in `source_url`.**
>
> Never infer an email from a domain name. Never pattern-guess `info@`.
> If it is not published, write `UNKNOWN`.

If a chain HQ email is the only contact for 12 clubs, record it **once** at HQ level and leave the club rows `UNKNOWN`. Do not replicate a guess twelve times.

## Scoring: who to visit first

| Score | Profile |
|---|---|
| **9–10** | Strength/bodybuilding gyms · CrossFit boxes · gyms with a **visible fridge or bar** · supplement stores · chains with many sites · university sport centres |
| **6–8** | General fitness gyms · martial arts and boxing · climbing gyms · padel and tennis clubs |
| **3–5** | Mixed studios with light retail |
| **1–2** | Yoga/pilates-only · tiny studios with no retail · hotel gyms |

**The single best predictor is whether they already sell drinks.** A gym with a fridge has already decided to be a retailer — you are only changing what is in it. A gym with no fridge is a much harder sell, regardless of size.

## What to do in the gym, not by email

Email gets ~10% replies from gym managers. Walking in gets ~70% conversations.

**Bring:** the one-page sell sheet, and (once you have them) cold cans.
**Ask for:** the manager or owner by name.
**Say the line from `27_GYM_SALES_STRATEGY.md`.**
**Leave with:** either a signed LOI or a written note of exactly why not.

> Both outcomes are valuable. A clear "no, because X" from ten gyms is worth more than a vague "maybe" from fifty.
