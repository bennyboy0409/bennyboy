# 27 — GYM SALES STRATEGY

**The offer a Hungarian gym will actually say yes to, and the words to say it in.**
1 EUR = 363 HUF. Batch 1 = **1,700 cans**, landed COGS **491 HUF (€1.35)/can**.

---

## THE OFFER — memorise this, it is the whole document

> **"You pay me nothing. I put a case in your fridge. You settle 639 Ft per can you actually sell, every two weeks. Whatever you charge above that is yours — I recommend 799 Ft, so that's 160 Ft a can in your pocket. If it doesn't move in 90 days I take it back at my cost."**

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| Gym pays upfront | **0 HUF / €0** |
| Model | **Consignment (bizomány)** — you keep title until the can is sold |
| Settlement price to you | **639 HUF (€1.76) gross per can sold** |
| Recommended RRP | **799 HUF (€2.20)** — gym keeps **160 HUF (€0.44) = 20%** |
| Price ceiling in the agreement | **899 HUF** — above that, members see three different prices in three gyms |
| Settlement cycle | **Every 14 days**, in person, physical count |
| First placement | **1–2 cases (24–48 cans)** |
| Unsold stock | You collect, at your cost, no charge to the gym |
| Payment terms | Cash or transfer **on settlement day**. Never 30 days. |
| Territory | Optional 90-day district exclusivity, free — see §11 |

**Do NOT open with "would you like to buy a case?"** At 491 HUF COGS you cannot sell a case profitably to a gym anyway (§3). The zero-risk placement is not a fallback — it is the product.

### The rule for the first 90 days

> **Sell only to people who can say yes in the room.**
> Every hour on a chain's procurement process is an hour not spent opening three independents.

### Franchise trap

For any chain site, establish **before** pitching whether it is company-owned or franchised. A franchisee often can buy freely; a company-owned club usually cannot. The `Impresszum` / `Kapcsolat` page names the operating legal entity — if the club's operating company differs from the brand's, it is a franchise and the manager may be able to sign.

### How Hungarian gyms buy drinks — **UNKNOWN**

`UNKNOWN — NEEDS PRIMARY RESEARCH.` Nobody in this project established whether Hungarian gyms buy direct, through a beverage wholesaler, or through a vending operator. `C7`, `C3`, `C5`, `C6` and `C8` all record this as their single biggest gap. **Three questions settle it and cost you nothing:**

1. *"Hogyan szoktak új terméket bevenni — megveszik, vagy bizományba?"*
2. *"Kitől veszik most az italt — nagykertől, közvetlenül, vagy automatás céggel dolgoznak?"*
3. *"Van kizárólagossági megállapodásuk bármelyik márkával?"*

**Question 3 matters most.** An exclusive pouring contract makes a venue unsellable regardless of product fit. Ask it in the first two minutes; it saves you the whole pitch.

## 3. WHY CONSIGNMENT, NOT WHOLESALE — the arithmetic that decides it

`DERIVED` — arithmetic shown, from batch-1 landed COGS **491 HUF/can** (`finance/22_PRICING.md`, `manufacturing/14_FIRST_BATCH.md`).

| Model | Gross in | less 27% ÁFA | Net to you | less COGS 491 | **Contribution/can** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wholesale to gym @ 470 net** | 470 net | — | 470 | −491 | **−21 HUF (−€0.06)** ❌ |
| **Consignment, gym keeps 160** | 639 | ÷1.27 → 503 | 503 | −491 | **+12 HUF (+€0.03)** ✅ |
| **Consignment, gym keeps 200 (25%)** | 599 | ÷1.27 → 472 | 472 | −491 | **−19 HUF** ❌ |
| **Direct sale at 799 (event, D2C, hand-sold)** | 799 | ÷1.27 → 629 | 629 | −491 | **+138 HUF (+€0.38)** ✅ |

> ⚠️ **Arithmetic note, read it.** The agreed plan states the consignment line as *"gym keeps 160, you net 511, +20 HUF/can."* Strict 27% ÁFA arithmetic on 639 HUF gross gives **503 net, +12 HUF**. The 511/+20 figure corresponds to a 150 HUF gym cut, or to rounding. **The truthful range is +12 to +20 HUF per can.** It does not change any decision — both are thin and positive, and both die at a 25% gym cut. **Hold the line at 20%.**
>
> **The number that would change everything:** if ÁFA is **18%** (milk-product rate) rather than 27%, 639 ÷ 1.18 = **541 net → +50 HUF/can (+€0.14)**, four times better. `NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION` — it is question 4 in `execution/34_OPEN_QUOTES.md` and it is answered by the same food-law opinion you are already buying.

### Six reasons consignment wins at 1,700 cans

1. **Wholesale is loss-making. There is no version of it that works at this batch size.** The choice is not consignment vs. profitable wholesale — it is consignment vs. no listing.
2. **It removes the gym's only real objection.** An unknown brand at a premium price is inventory risk. Zero cash out = nothing to refuse.
3. **You keep title, so you can move stock.** A dead account's case goes into a live account's fridge next week. Under wholesale it sits in someone else's fridge until it expires.
4. **You get per-account sell-through data.** That is the asset that makes conversation #2 — with a distributor, a chain, or an investor — possible at all (`C7` §8.3). Sell-in data proves nothing; sell-through proves everything.
5. **It is the only structure that fits your cash position.** You cannot finance receivables with €1,000 (`C2` §7, `C8` §4.4, independently).
6. **It caps your downside per account at one case** — 24 cans, 11,784 HUF (€32.5) of COGS.

### The two real costs of consignment — do not pretend these away

- **You carry the inventory and the risk.** If nothing sells, you own 1,700 unsold cans and the loss is total.
- **It costs founder-hours.** Each account needs a physical visit every 14 days: count, restock, settle, invoice. Budget **30–45 minutes per account per fortnight** including travel. **At 15 accounts that is roughly one full day every two weeks.** That, not market size, is what caps your account count (`C8` §9.5: founder-hours are the binding constraint).

### The signalling risk, and how to defuse it

`C8` §4.4 warns: *"Never offer consignment as your opening terms. It signals you expect the product not to sell."* That warning is correct **when profitable wholesale is available**. It is not, here. Defuse the signal with framing:

- Do **not** call it *bizomány*, *sale-or-return* or *visszárus*. Call it a **"90-day launch placement" (90 napos bevezető kihelyezés)**.
- State the expiry out loud: **"This is how I launch batch 1. From batch 2 it becomes a normal wholesale line at 470–520 Ft."** That makes it a launch mechanic, not a weakness.
- Pair it with the exclusivity offer (§11). You are giving something, not begging.

**Wholesale becomes viable at roughly 25,000 cans/year.** Note what else crosses at that volume: **above ~5,000 cans/year the Hungarian DRS deposit system applies** — 50 HUF deposit, MOHU registration, DRS mark on the artwork, deposit float. Do not sign a wholesale deal that pushes you over 5,000 units before the legal opinion lands.**

The one quantified Hungarian retail-margin figure anyone found (28.6–40%) came from a vocational-training PDF, is not beverage-specific and is undated. **Do not plan on it. Ask five owners instead.**

## 5. CASE SIZE, SAMPLES, PAYMENT TERMS

### Case

| | |
|---|---|
| **Standard case** | **24 cans** — the natural trade unit for a 330 ml can |
| Half case | **12 cans** — the "just try it" unit for a nervous first account. Costs you nothing but packing time |
| Batch 1 total | 1,700 cans ≈ **71 cases** |
| Your COGS per case | **11,784 HUF (€32.5)** |
| Retail value of a case @ 799 | 19,176 HUF (€52.8) |
| Gym's cash if all 24 sell @ 799 | **3,840 HUF (€10.6) per case** |

> **Be honest with yourself about that last number.** At 50 cans/month a gym earns **8,000 HUF (€22)/month** from you. That is not a business case for them, and if you pitch it as one you will lose credibility. **Pitch it as: costs you nothing, takes one shelf slot, and your members have been asking for a protein drink that isn't a milkshake.** The money argument is for batch 3, when they are buying wholesale and the numbers are real.

### Free samples — hard cap

`DERIVED` at 491 HUF/can.

| Purpose | Cans | Cost HUF | EUR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prospecting visits — **2 cans per gym × 20 gyms** | 40 | 19,640 | 54 |
| Staff tasting at signed accounts — 4 cans × 6 best accounts | 24 | 11,784 | 32 |
| Events / competitions sampling | 16 | 7,856 | 22 |
| **TOTAL CAP** | **80 cans = 4.7% of batch** | **39,280** | **108** |

**Rules:**
- **No prospect gets more than 4 free cans before signing.** Two for the decision-maker, two for the coach who will actually recommend it.
- **A gym asking for "a case to try" is asking you to fund its trial.** The answer is not no — the answer is: *"That's exactly what the placement is. You pay nothing for the case. You only settle what sells."* **Consignment IS the free trial. Never give away a case.**
- Never exceed 5% of the batch on free product.

### Payment terms

| Situation | Terms | Non-negotiable |
|---|---|---|
| **Consignment settlement** | **Cash or transfer on the settlement visit, every 14 days** | ✅ |
| First 3 orders from any gym that wants to **buy** outright | **Prepayment, or 8 days from delivery** | ✅ |
| Order 4+ from a gym with a clean payment record | 8–15 days | negotiable |
| **A new gym asking for 30 days** | **No.** | ✅ **Never** |

> **The script for the 30-day ask:** *„Harminc napos fizetést nem tudok adni, mert az első gyártást a saját pénzemből fizetem. De pont ezért nem is kérek Öntől semmit előre — csak azt fizeti ki, ami tényleg elfogyott."*
> **A founder who cannot say no to payment terms runs out of cash before batch 2.** With consignment you never have to have this conversation — that is a reason to prefer it, not a coincidence.

### Paperwork per placement — 4 items, all one page

1. **Átadás-átvételi jegyzék** (delivery/handover note): date, gym, quantity, **batch number**, signature. Batch number is a legal traceability requirement, not admin.
2. **The placement agreement** — one page: settlement price 639 HUF/can sold, recommended RRP 799, ceiling 899, 14-day settlement, 90-day review, you collect unsold at your cost.
3. **Settlement sheet** per visit: opening count, closing count, sold, amount due.
4. **Invoice** for cans sold. ⚠️ **`NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION` — ask your accountant how to invoice a consignment (*bizományosi*) arrangement in Hungary and when the ÁFA liability arises.** The simple version — you invoice the gym 639 HUF gross per can sold at settlement — is probably right and is what the numbers above assume, but the timing rules are specific. Also confirm whether cash collection at gyms requires an online **pénztárgép** (item 14 in `execution/34_OPEN_QUOTES.md`). **Take transfers, not cash, until that is answered.**

## 6. HOW MANY CANS WILL A GYM ACTUALLY SELL

`ESTIMATE — derivation shown`, from `C8_hu_fitness_market.md` §9. Inputs: mid-size Hungarian gym ≈ **150 check-ins/day × 26 days = 3,900 visits/month**.

| Scenario | Attach rate | **Cans/month** | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold shelf** — in the fridge, no support, staff indifferent | 0.5% | **~20** | The default for a listing with nothing behind it |
| **Realistic steady state** ⭐ | 1.0–1.5% | **40–60** | **Plan on this** |
| **Good account** — engaged owner, sampling done, staff recommending | 2.0–3.0% | **80–120** | Achievable, costs you founder-hours per account |
| **Flagship** — 400+ check-ins/day, real bar | — | 200–400 | Rare. Assume zero |
| **Month 1, any account** | — | **20–30** | Nobody knows the product yet |

**Sanity check:** 40–60 cans/month is 1.5–2.3 cans/day in a gym seeing 150 visits/day — about **one member in 70** buying one can. For an unfamiliar premium product that is reasonable.

> **One upward correction to the C8 model, in your favour.** Those attach rates were derived against an assumed gym-bar price of **~1,400 HUF**. Your RRP is **799 HUF**. At roughly half the assumed price the barrier is lower, so **40–60/month is if anything conservative**. Do not bank the difference — measure it at your first account.

### How many gyms to move 1,700 cans in 90 days

`DERIVED` using C8's portfolio model: 20% strong (250 cans/90 days), 50% medium (120), 30% weak (24, no reorder) → weighted **117 cans/account/90 days**, less a **35% ramp haircut** for staggered onboarding → **76 cans per account inside the window**.

| Route | Accounts needed | Achievable solo? |
|---|---|---|
| Sell-in only, 1 case each (24 cans) | 1,700 ÷ 24 = **71 accounts** | ❌ No |
| **Gyms only, blended sell-through** | 1,700 ÷ 76 = **23 accounts** | ⚠️ Tight but possible |
| **RECOMMENDED BLEND** ⭐ | **12–15 gym accounts (~1,000–1,100 cans) + ~600–700 cans direct** | ✅ Yes |

**Open ~20–25 accounts to finish with 15 active ones.** Roughly a third of anything you open will not reorder — that is built into the model, not a failure.

Field-sales reality (`C8` §9.5): **2–4 new accounts per week** including travel, waiting for the owner, the pitch and the follow-up. Over 12 weeks that is 24–48 accounts *if you do nothing else*. You will also be managing production, DRS, labelling and school. **Plan for the bottom of that range.**

### Where the other 600–700 cans go — at full 138 HUF (€0.38) contribution

| Channel | Cans | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Mass-participation sport events** (running races, CrossFit comps, strength meets) | 200–400 | Sell at **full RRP with no channel margin given away** — the best cans-per-hour in the whole plan (`C8` §7.3). Often free to attend as a product sponsor rather than a paying exhibitor |
| **D2C, multi-packs only** | 100–200 | Full margin, plus customer emails you own. Single-can shipping is economically absurd |
| Hand-sold: your own gym, your team, university sport days | 100–200 | Zero acquisition cost |

**Skip trade fairs.** A HUNGEXPO-scale stand plausibly consumes the entire capital. Participation events invert the economics in your favour.

## 7. WHERE THE PROSPECTS COME FROM

### AllYouCanMove and MultiSport — **prospect directories, NOT channels**

`STRUCTURAL ANALYSIS` (`C8` §2.3). **This is the single most common mistake and it is worth being clear about:**

> A sports-pass aggregator sells access subscriptions and pays venues per check-in. **It does not buy inventory, hold stock, run a warehouse, or resell products.** There is no purchasing department to sell 3,000 cans to. **Pitching "we'd like to be listed by AYCM" marks you as someone who hasn't understood their business. Do not send them a wholesale pitch.**

**What they are worth instead:** their partner-venue directories are the densest, highest-quality free prospect list in Hungary — and better than a generic gym directory in a specific way. A venue that joined AYCM or MultiSport has already (i) negotiated a third-party commercial agreement, (ii) accepted a partner's terms, (iii) set up recurring invoicing to a corporate counterparty, and (iv) decided extra footfall is worth a margin concession. **All four are preconditions for stocking a new supplier.** A venue on both networks is doubly qualified — and it will not be spooked by a delivery note and an invoice.

⚠️ **URL warning.** `aycm.hu` was attempted in research and returned a **proxy policy block, which is not confirmation the site exists**. It is an **UNVERIFIED CANDIDATE** — find it in a browser, do not print it on anything. **No MultiSport URL is recorded anywhere in this project and none has been guessed.** Find it from Benefit Systems' own site or from any partner gym's page, where the accepted-pass logos are normally linked.

**Marketing partnership with either network: correct sequencing is to build 20–30 venue relationships first, then open with *"we're already in 30 of your partner venues."*** That conversation has leverage. Day-one has none.

### The rest of the prospect stack

Full method in `sales/26_GYM_PROSPECTS_HOWTO.md`. Priority: AYCM/MultiSport directories → **CrossFit official affiliate map (`map.crossfit.com`)** → chain *Klubjaink* pages → Google Maps by district → supplement stores → university sport centres (TF first).

**The single best predictor of a yes: does the venue already have a fridge?** A gym with a fridge has already decided to be a retailer — you are only changing what is in it.

### The one named target worth a chain-level approach

**Life1 Fitness** — the only Hungarian operator with *confirmed* in-club drink retail (the "Protein Bar", `SEARCH-VERIFIED`). Priority 10 in `26_GYM_PROSPECTS.csv`. **Approach HQ, not individual clubs.** Expect a slow answer; send it in week 1 and work independents while you wait. ## 8. THE VISIT

### The 30-second pitch — Hungarian, say it out loud until it is natural

> „Jó napot! [NÉV] vagyok, egy magyar fehérjeital-márkát indítok. 330 ml-es doboz, szénsavas, cukormentes, **20 gramm fehérje** — úgy iszod, mint egy üdítőt, nem úgy, mint egy turmixot.
>
> Az első gyártás 1 700 doboz, és 12–15 termet keresek, aki kirakja a hűtőbe. **Nem kell érte fizetnie előre.** Kihelyezem, 799 forint a javasolt fogyasztói ár, és **minden eladott dobozon 160 forint marad Önöknél**. Kéthetente jövök, megszámoljuk, és csak az elfogyott dobozokat számlázom. Ha 90 nap alatt nem fogy, elviszem, az én költségemen.
>
> **Van két perce?"**

### The closing question — this is the one that produces a decision

> „Ha most a kezébe adnék 24 dobozt, amiért nem fizet semmit, és minden eladott dobozon 160 forint maradna Önnél — kirakná a hűtőbe?"

A yes to that is an account. A "maybe, send me an email" is a no — treat it as one, and move on.

## 9. OBJECTION HANDLING — scripted

**Rule: answer in one or two sentences, then ask a question back.** Long answers read as anxiety.

| # | Objection (as you'll hear it) | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | „Már veszünk a BioTechUSA-tól / Scitectől." | **Category, never price.** „Ez nem fehérjeturmix és nem porkiegészítő — ez egy üdítő. Nem a polcon a versenytársuk, hanem a hűtőben, a Red Bull mellett. Más vásárlási pillanat." |
| **2** | „Drága. 799 forint sok egy dobozért." | „Fehérjeporban egy adag 790 forint — és azt még el kell keverni. **Nulla forint különbség, csak ez kész van.** Egy Red Bull 549, és nulla gramm fehérje van benne." |
| **3** | „Nem akarok pénzt kötni bele." | „Nem is kell. **Nulla forint.** Csak azt fizeti ki, ami elfogyott, kéthetente. Ami nem fogy, azt elviszem." **This is the whole offer — if you hear this objection you buried the lede. Lead with it next time.** |
| **4** | „Nincs hely a hűtőben." | „Egy karton, egy polcrész. Ha két hét alatt nem mozdul, elviszem és nem beszélünk róla többet." Then offer the top shelf at eye level — ask, don't assume. |

| **9** | „Küldjön egy árlistát / írjon e-mailt." | The brush-off. „Küldök. De hagyok itt két dobozt — kóstolja meg az edzőkkel. **Csütörtökön ugyanekkor visszajövök, és akkor csak annyit mondjon: igen vagy nem.**" Name the day. |
| **10** | „Kaphatnék kizárólagosságot a kerületben?" | **Yes — say yes fast, it costs nothing.** „Igen. 90 napra, írásban, a kerületben Ön az egyetlen. Utána közösen megnézzük a számokat." |
| **11** | „Harminc nap fizetési határidő." | „Nem tudok adni, az első gyártást a saját pénzemből fizetem. **De épp ezért nem is kérek semmit előre.**" |
| **12** | „Mennyit lehet ezen keresni?" | Do not oversell. „Havi 40–60 doboz reális egy ilyen méretű teremnél — az **6 400–9 600 forint**. Nem ettől lesz jobb a hónapjuk. **Attól lesz, hogy van a hűtőben valami, amit máshol nem kapnak meg.**" |
| **13** | „Kell rá visszaváltási díj / betétdíj?" | **Say nothing until the legal opinion lands.** If asked directly: „Ezt most tisztázza az ügyvédem, és amint megvan, írásban megküldöm." Never assert the DRS position to a customer before it is confirmed (`legal/10_DRS_REPONT.md`). |
| **14** | „Ki szállítja? Mennyi a minimum?" | „Én szállítom, személyesen. **Nincs minimum.** Egy karton is jó." |

### The three things that lose the deal

1. **Leading with the money.** 8,000 HUF/month is not persuasive. Novelty, zero risk and exclusivity are.
2. **Guessing an answer.** „Nem tudom, de utánanézek és holnap megírom" is a stronger answer than a confident wrong one — and in a small gym community a wrong answer travels.
3. **Leaving without a date.** Every visit ends with either a signature or a named day you come back.

## 10. WHAT TO TRACK — one row per account, per visit

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Date placed, cans placed, batch number | Legal traceability + your inventory |
| **Days to first reorder** ⭐ | **The single most important number in the business.** If your first ten accounts don't reorder, you don't have a distribution problem — you have a product or price problem, and adding accounts spreads the failure wider |
| Cans sold per 14-day cycle | The only real demand signal |
| Shelf price the gym actually charges | Free primary research into real willingness-to-pay. If several gyms price at 899 and still sell, your RRP is too low |
| Objection given, verbatim | Patterns across 20 gyms tell you what to fix in batch 2 |
| Fridge position (eye level / bottom / back) | Explains half your variance between accounts |

> **Ten accounts with a proven reorder rate are worth more than forty without one** — and they are the only evidence that will ever raise money or open a chain.

## 11. THE FREE CONCESSIONS — give these, they cost nothing

| Concession | Cost to you | When to play it |
|---|---|---|
| **90-day district exclusivity, in writing** | 0 HUF | Whenever asked, and volunteer it to your first 5 accounts |
| Free branded shelf card / fridge sticker | print cost | At signing |
| Named as a launch partner on your Instagram | 0 HUF | At signing — gyms like this more than you'd expect |
| **You collect unsold stock at your cost** | ~1 case risk | In the agreement from the start, it is the whole offer |
| A personal discount code for their coaches | margin on a few cans | To convert the coach who will actually recommend it |
| Delivery in person, any quantity, no minimum | your time | Always |

**Never concede:** the 20% cut (25% is loss-making), 30-day terms, a free case, or a wholesale price in batch 1.
