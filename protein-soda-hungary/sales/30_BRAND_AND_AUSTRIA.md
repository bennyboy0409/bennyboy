### Leave OFF the front
Any second functional claim (leucine, tryptophan, BCAA, electrolytes, vitamins) · a tagline (nobody reads one at 2 m) · certification logos, vegan/gluten-free/lactose-free (side panel — these are pickup-moment information) · founder story · fruit photography (reads as squash and drags you downmarket next to NOCCO) · **the DRS/deposit mark — see §7.**

### The discipline to copy
One layout, one grid, **one colour per flavour**, and a refusal to add a fourth front message. Three future SKUs on one grid in three colours = a shelf block that reads as a brand. Three SKUs with three layouts = three failed experiments. **This is why the sleeve template matters more than the first flavour.**

## 3. COLLAGEN: prominent or secondary? — **SECONDARY. Off the front. Fully disclosed on the back.**

### The decision

> ### 🔴 CORRECTION — THE LEUCINE FIGURE COMES OFF THE PACK ENTIRELY IN BATCH 1
> An earlier draft instructed printing **"3 g leucine per can"** on the side panel. **Do not print it anywhere on the can in batch 1.** Three independent reasons, any one of which is sufficient:
> 1. **Reg. (EC) 1924/2006 Art. 8(1) operates a closed list.** A leucine-content claim is not in the Annex, so it is prohibited **however true it is** — the same defect this project used to kill *"alacsony szénhidráttartalmú"*.
> 2. `legal/09_LABEL_COMPLIANCE.md` §8 separately warns that a front-panel `+ LEUCIN` flash **triggers QUID** and instructs "Batch 1: keep off the front panel."
> 3. **The legality of adding free L-leucine to a normal beverage at all is UNKNOWN** under Reg. (EC) 1925/2006 and is flagged as **blocking raw-material purchase**. If the answer is no, the leucine is not in the can and the figure was never printable.
>
> **Where it may still be used:** spoken, in the room, and as **off-pack factual copy** on the sell sheet and product page — **and only once the Art. 8(1) question and the free-amino-acid legality question are both answered.** Until then it is a talking point, not a claim. `NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION.`

| Where | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Front of can** | Nothing about protein source. "20 G PROTEIN" only. |
| **Side/back panel** | Full precise disclosure: *whey protein isolate, hydrolysed bovine collagen peptides, L-leucine, L-tryptophan*, with grams, **as an ingredient list — not as a leucine claim.** No "3 g leucine" figure on the pack in batch 1. |
| **Sell sheet + gym-owner conversation** | **This is where the leucine answer lives** — off-pack, factual, and only after the two legality questions above are answered. This is where the objection is actually raised and where you can answer it. |
| **Instagram / product page** | Full formula, openly. One post explaining *why* the blend. Off-pack copy is subject to the same claims regulation as the label — **do not treat social as a free space.** |

### Why secondary — four reasons

**1. Your channel is the exact audience that penalises collagen.** You are selling through gyms, studios and supplement shops — the most protein-literate segment there is. The objection is substantive, not noise: **collagen is an incomplete protein (no tryptophan) and is low in leucine**, the amino acid that drives muscle protein synthesis. A gym buyer who sees "COLLAGEN" next to "20 G PROTEIN" does not read a bonus — **they read a downgrade, and worse, they read it as inflating a protein number with a cheaper protein.** That turns your headline claim from an asset into an argument, and you are not standing at the fridge to win it.

**2. The gatekeeper is more literate than the consumer.** The buying decision in your channel is made by a gym owner or shop manager who has heard every protein pitch. **A collagen-forward pitch invites the leucine question in the first meeting.** Fine — as long as you have the answer, which you do (below), and as long as the can hasn't already lost the argument for you.

**3. The scepticism is now published, not just folkloric.** Health Club Management ran a piece casting doubt on collagen for muscle building — **SEARCH-VERIFIED per the project brief; not present in the C9 dossier and not re-verified here. Do not quote this publication to a customer without reading the actual article.** Directionally it tells you the argument has escaped the forums and reached trade press your gym owners read.

**4. Asymmetric downside.** Leading with collagen and being wrong costs you the gym channel — your only channel. Keeping it secondary and being wrong costs you upside with a beauty/wellness audience you cannot reach anyway (that audience lives in pharmacy, drugstore and beauty retail, not gyms — different product, different channel, different year). **Take the cheap mistake.**

### Why this is NOT dishonest, and why you are still safe

The dossier's harder line was: *"Do not print 20 G PROTEIN as the hero if it is collagen."* **ARCH F is not collagen. It is a 50/50 blend with the incomplete half deliberately repaired.** Three facts:

**a) The leucine is spiked to a level the objection cannot survive.**

| 10 g whey protein isolate | ~1.0–1.2 g | **ESTIMATE** — WPI is typically ~10–12% leucine by weight. **QUOTE REQUIRED: read the actual % off your supplier's CoA/spec sheet before printing any number.** |
| 10 g hydrolysed bovine collagen | ~0.2–0.3 g | **ESTIMATE**, same caveat |

**~3 g leucine per serving is the threshold figure the whole muscle-protein-synthesis argument is conducted around.** Once you can state it, "but collagen is low in leucine" stops being an objection about your product and becomes a general remark about collagen. **This is the single most valuable sentence in your sell sheet — spoken and off-pack only, per the correction above, and only once the CoAs and the two legality questions land.**

⚠️ **Note what the research actually says about leucine.** Dossier A5's only mention of leucine is a **warning** (§8.4) that collagen's low leucine content makes muscle-mass claims a scrutiny risk. **Nothing in the research base supports a leucine claim.** The ~3 g total is our own arithmetic off two ESTIMATE rows, and it is an argument you make in a conversation, not a claim you underwrite.

**b) The tryptophan gap is closed on purpose.** 150 mg L-tryptophan is in the formula precisely because collagen contains none. "Incomplete protein" is the standard objection; you have completed it. Say so, in one line, on the back. ⚠️ **The dose itself is unsettled:** dossier D2 §8 specifies **25 mg added** tryptophan (a 15 mg deficit plus analytical margin); the deliverables carry **150 mg**. `manufacturing/16_INGREDIENT_SUPPLIERS.csv` flags the conflict. Cost impact is trivial (0.59 HUF/can) but **the co-packer doses what the spec sheet says and the label declares it — settle the number before the RFQ goes out.**

**c) A named competitor already does this openly in your market.** **Myprotein sells a whey + collagen clear protein product in Hungary, openly — SEARCH-VERIFIED per the project brief; not in the C9 dossier, not re-verified here.** If that holds, the blend is not a category taboo in Hungary and you are not pioneering an unsellable structure. **Test it yourself for free: walk into a supplement shop and look at the label.** That single observation is worth more than any further research.

**None of that changes the front-of-can call.** A back panel can carry a three-step argument. A can at 2 metres cannot. The front's job is to get picked up; the back's job is to survive scrutiny; the sell sheet's job is to beat the gatekeeper. **Different surfaces, different jobs.**

### The one alternative, and the test that settles it

**Option B:** add a single small front line under ZERO SUGAR — `WHEY ISOLATE + COLLAGEN`. Gains pre-emptive transparency; costs you a fourth message and roughly halves the impact of "20 G". **I do not recommend it.** (An earlier version of Option B appended `· 3 G LEUCINE`. **The test (cost: the price of a few cans):** tape two printed mock-up wraps — recommended vs Option B — to real cans, stand in three Budapest gyms, ask twenty people which they'd buy and why. **This is worth more than every piece of secondary research in this project and it is nearly free.** Do it before you pay for final artwork.

### Non-negotiable: never conceal

**The fitness audience punishes concealment far harder than it punishes collagen.** A discovered omission reads as deception and is unrecoverable. Full ingredient honesty on the back, silence on the front — that is a hierarchy decision, not a hiding decision, and if anyone asks, say exactly that.

**Open legal point — NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION:** whether collagen counts toward the declared "protein" figure and toward the EU "high protein" claim (Reg. 1924/2006). Under FIC 1169/2011 protein is calculated as total nitrogen × 6.25, which would include collagen — **LIKELY, but this is model reasoning, not a sourced finding, and it underwrites your entire front-of-can claim. Get it confirmed by the co-packer's label consultant before artwork is finalised.**

⚠️ **The "MAGAS FEHÉRJETARTALMÚ" claim itself is `LIKELY — NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION`, not confirmed.** Two founder-facing files tag it CONFIRMED "via Reg. 1924/2006 Annex"; **no Annex text was ever read — EUR-Lex was 403-blocked**, and dossier A5 tags the ≥20%-of-energy condition `LIKELY — UNVERIFIED`. **Do not let the strongest tag in the set sit on the one claim the whole front panel is built around.** The arithmetic is comfortable — 20 g × 4 kcal = **80 kcal of protein**, essentially the whole energy content of a zero-sugar can (**DERIVED**) — and the claim holds at any protein level down to 12 g **provided total can energy stays below 240 kcal**. ### The free clearance protocol — 30 minutes, 0 HUF. Do this before any artwork.

1. **Class 32** is your class (non-alcoholic beverages incl. sports drinks). If powder is ever possible, also check **class 5**. **Treat class 32 as decisive.**
2. **TMview** (`tmview.org`) — aggregates EUIPO + national offices + WIPO. **Fastest way to sweep 15 names.**
3. **EUIPO eSearch** (`euipo.europa.eu`) — each name as a word mark, exact string **and** obvious variants. Read class 5 / 29 / 30 hits too — an adjacent food-class mark can still be cited against you. **A dead/expired mark is not a blocker; a pending application is.**
4. **SZTNH** (`sztnh.gov.hu`) — Hungarian national register. **A Hungarian national mark in class 32 blocks you at home even if EUIPO is clear.**
5. **Real-world check** — name + "drink"/"protein" on plain web search and on Instagram. An unregistered brand already trading in your category is a practical problem regardless of the register.
6. **Log every check** — date, database, class, result, application number of any conflict. **The log is your good-faith clearance evidence if you are ever challenged.**

# PART 2 — AUSTRIA

## 8. Verdict

### **DO NOT ENTER AUSTRIA IN YEAR 1. HUNGARY ONLY.**

Five reasons, in order of force:

1. **Capital.** The Austrian **fixed** entry cost is the same order of magnitude as the founder's entire capital base. At the optimistic end it is a material fraction of it; at the pessimistic end it exceeds it. **Spending your only money on regulatory registrations in a market with no customer is the wrong use of the last euro you control.**
2. **The authorised-representative risk (§12).** If Austria requires a domestic *Bevollmächtigter* for packaging obligations, that is a **recurring annual retainer that does not scale down to 1,700 cans** and it settles the question by itself. **This is one email and it may end the discussion in a week.**
3. **Doubling the admin before validating once.** Two deposit registrations, two EPR registrations, two sleeves, probably two GTINs — before a single Hungarian gym has **reordered**. **Reordering is the only signal that matters in year 1, and none of the Austrian work generates it.**
4. **The economics run backwards at your size.** Every Austrian fixed cost gets cheaper per can with scale. You have no scale. Entering Austria is a scale play executed pre-scale.
5. **Nothing is lost by waiting.** The Austrian system is not closing. The registrations are equally available in year 2, done faster, cheaper per can, with a validated product.

**The one condition that flips this:** an Austrian distributor or gym chain approaches **you** with a committed order large enough to amortise the fixed cost — realistically five figures of cans. **Do not go looking for it; do take the call if it comes.**

## 12. Austrian packaging EPR (ARA) and the representative question

**The obligation (UNVERIFIED — model knowledge):** Austria operates packaging producer responsibility under the *Verpackungsverordnung*. Anyone first placing packaging on the Austrian market must join a licensed collection-and-recovery scheme. **Altstoff Recycling Austria AG (ARA)** is the largest and best known, **but the household packaging market was liberalised and competing schemes exist — ARA is the default, not the only option, so you can quote-shop.** → verify at `ara.at`.

**Do not skip this because of the deposit system.** Whether your deposit-bearing cans are carved out of the ARA obligation now that the deposit runs is **UNKNOWN — a specific question to ask.** But your **secondary and tertiary packaging definitely is not**: shrink film around multipacks, cardboard trays and cases, pallets and stretch wrap, D2C shipping cartons and void fill. All of it carries a weight-based EPR fee. **At your volumes the weights are tiny and the fees will be small — but the registration is a fixed obligation regardless, and it is one more counterparty.**

### **Bevollmächtigter — the most likely single dealbreaker**

**UNVERIFIED — model knowledge, and it is the highest-stakes item here.** The general EU pattern is that a producer **without establishment** in a Member State must appoint an **authorised representative** established there to discharge EPR obligations. **Austria has historically been among the stricter Member States on requiring foreign distance sellers and foreign producers to have a domestic representative for packaging obligations.**

**An authorised representative is a paid service with an annual retainer, and retainers do not scale down to 1,700 cans.** A retainer in the low hundreds to low thousands of euro per year — **the figure is UNKNOWN** — would consume a crippling share of, or exceed, the entire project budget, for a market with zero validated demand.

**This, not the Lizenzentgelt, is the most likely single reason Austria is uneconomic in year 1. It is also the fastest question to get answered.**

## 13. Austrian food business registration

**UNKNOWN in every operational detail** — `ages.at` was blocked, no forms, no authority routing, no fees. What follows is **UNVERIFIED — model knowledge**, offered only so you know what to ask:

- Austria implements **Reg. (EC) 852/2004**: every food business operator must be **registered with the competent authority for each establishment under its control** — the same EU legal root as the Hungarian requirement.
- The registration counterparty is generally the **Bezirksverwaltungsbehörde** (district administrative authority; the Magistrat in statutory cities such as Vienna). **AGES does risk assessment and testing — it is generally NOT the body you register with.** Verify this.
- **Registration attaches to an ESTABLISHMENT — premises where you handle food. It is place-based, not a sell-into-the-country obligation.**

**The question that actually matters: does a Hungarian company selling INTO Austria need Austrian food registration?**

**The principle (UNVERIFIED):** under EU free movement of goods, a food lawfully produced and marketed in one Member State can generally be sold in another without a second national authorisation, and **the 852/2004 obligation follows your establishments, not your customers.** Hungarian company + Hungarian co-packer + no Austrian premises ⇒ on this principle you register where your establishments physically are, **not additionally in Austria merely because Austrian shops sell your can.**

**Where that stops — check these hardest:**
1. **Take a warehouse or any stockholding point in Austria and you have an Austrian establishment.** The analysis changes.
2. The **"responsible food business operator" on the label** is a separate obligation and bites regardless (§14).
3. **⚠️ Deposit and EPR obligations do not follow food-law logic at all.** They are triggered by *placing packaging on the Austrian market*, which you unambiguously do the moment an Austrian shop sells your can. **This is the crucial asymmetry: you may need NO Austrian food registration and still need Austrian deposit registration AND Austrian EPR registration. Do not let a reassuring answer on food law lull you on packaging law.**
4. **Selling D2C from Hungary to Austrian consumers is a different fact pattern** — distance selling changes VAT (OSS) and typically pulls EPR and deposit obligations onto the seller directly.

**Action:** put this question in writing to the **WKO (Austrian Chamber of Commerce)** — it runs free enquiry services for exactly this and is the cheapest route to a written answer. **Cost of Austrian food registration itself: UNKNOWN. Do not assume zero; do not assume large.**
