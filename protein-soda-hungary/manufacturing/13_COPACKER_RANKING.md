## 1. THE STRUCTURAL FINDING — why this product barely exists in Europe

This is worth more than any supplier name, and it explains every dead end you are about to hit.

> **"Dairy-approved" and "carbonated aluminium can" are two separate industrial worlds in Europe, and they barely overlap. Your product sits in the gap.**

Two industries, two hygiene regimes, two capital stacks:

| | The dairy world | The can world |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Molkerei Gropper, Ehrmann, Müller/Sachsenmilch, Hochwald, DMK, Alföldi Tej, Sole-Mizó | RHODIUS, Rauch, Starzinger, Egger, Bubee, BeverageScouts, Bayerndose |
| Approved for products of animal origin | **Yes** | Almost never |
| Fills **carbonated** liquid | **No** — protein RTDs are still | **Yes** |
| Fills **aluminium cans** | **No evidence found for any of them** | Yes, it is their entire business |
| Result | Ehrmann ships a **20 g protein drink** — still, in a bottle | RHODIUS ships **600 m cans/year** — none of them contain protein |

**Evidence:** sibling dossier B2 tested the "big private-label dairy house" hypothesis directly against Gropper, Ehrmann, Müller/Sachsenmilch, Weihenstephan, Hochwald, BMI, Milchwerke Schwaben and DMK/Milram. **Not one was substantiated as a carbonated-can co-packer.** Gropper's High Protein Drink is 332 ml — *in a bottle*. `SEARCH-VERIFIED (B2 §3.9, B7 §3.7)`

**The consequence for you:** the rare bridges are disproportionately valuable — a **can filler that names protein**. In all of Hungary, Austria and Germany, exactly three companies published anything close:

- **Füllkopf (DE)** — own site names *"protein drinks"* and *"BCAA"* as product categories. IFS + BIO. **10,000-can minimum.**
- **Bubee (HU)** — explicitly offers contract manufacture of *BCAA drinks, sports drinks, functional drinks*. **MOQ unpublished.**
- **BeverageScouts (AT)** — recipe development for beverages *and liquid food supplements*. **Test fillings from 1,000 cans.**

### 1.1 …and collagen sidesteps the whole problem

Hydrolysed collagen peptides are technically a product of animal origin, but commercially they behave like a shelf-stable powdered ingredient: **low microbial load, acid-stable, dissolves clear at pH 3.2, no haze, no gelling, no foam like whey, and no milk allergen declaration.** For a filler, adding a collagen powder is a far smaller change to its HACCP plan than admitting a dairy allergen to the site.

**That is precisely why Opre' can list "collagen beverages" and no Hungarian filler lists whey protein sodas.** It is also why every shipped European carbonated "protein" product is collagen or BCAA: Ölgerðin's Mist Uppbygging 15 g hydrolysed collagen, Collab 5.9 g marine collagen, NOCCO ~3 g as BCAA. **Nobody in Europe ships 20 g of complete protein in a carbonated can.**

### 1.2 ⚠️ THE CATCH YOU MUST NOT MISS — ARCH F IS A HYBRID

**Formula ARCH F is 10 g collagen + 10 g whey protein isolate. It is not a collagen drink.**

The 10 g of WPI:
- **halves** the whey load, the haze risk and the foaming penalty versus a 20 g all-whey formula (ARCH E, correctly rejected);
- **does not remove the milk allergen**, so allergen changeover validation and a milk declaration on the sleeve still apply;
- **does not remove the dairy-establishment question** — it only makes it smaller.

**Therefore: ask every candidate for a two-line quote — ARCH F as specified (10 g collagen + 10 g WPI), AND a collagen-only fallback.** If a filler that is otherwise perfect says "no dairy on site", the collagen-only version is your escape hatch and it costs you the amino profile, not the batch. Have that fallback formula ready *before* you send the RFQ, not after the rejection.

## 3. THE CRAFT-BREWERY INSIGHT — and its built-in contradiction

**The thesis, sourced:** Forbes.hu, *"A Nagy Magyar Craft Üdítő Üzlet"* `LIKELY (secondary source)`:

> Modern craft breweries operate at **20–40% of capacity**, with plenty of spare capacity; for a larger one producing 500,000 litres of beer, manufacturing **2,000–3,000 litres of soft drink is almost unnoticed.**

And from the same piece — **the only Hungarian contract-filling cost anchor that exists anywhere in this entire research set**:

> Generally **2,000 litres can be produced for HUF 1–1.5 million, which yields about 6,000 bottles** of soft drink.

`DERIVED`: 1,000,000 ÷ 6,000 = **167 HUF/unit (EUR 0.46)**; 1,500,000 ÷ 6,000 = **250 HUF/unit (EUR 0.69)**. Batch total **EUR 2,755–4,132**. **This is a simple craft lemonade. Use it as a floor, never as a quote for your product.**

**This is why the Hungarian influencer-soda boom happened, and it is your opening.** Hungary's craft breweries have idle carbonation and filling capacity and a structural incentive to sell it cheap. **MONYO has already productised it as *üdítő bérgyártás*.** HopTop sells small runs as *"gerilla"* brewing. Fehér Nyúl has ~200,000 L of fermentation capacity.

> ### ⚠️ AND HERE IS THE CENTRAL STRATEGIC TENSION OF THIS ENTIRE PROJECT
> **The craft brewery channel is the low-MOQ route in Hungary — and it is also the channel least likely to have dairy approval or any protein-handling experience.**
>
> A brewery filling a whey-containing drink faces three real, chargeable obstacles: (1) **establishment scope** — an establishment handling products of animal origin sits in a different approval class under EU food hygiene law, and a brewery registered for beer and soft drinks is very unlikely to be *approved* for a dairy-derived raw material `LIKELY — NEEDS PROFESSIONAL CONFIRMATION WITH NÉBIH`; (2) **allergen changeover** — milk is one of the 14 declarable EU allergens, and introducing it forces new cleaning validation, swab testing and an updated HACCP plan, which a brewery will price into your run or refuse; (3) **foaming** — 20 g/can in a counter-pressure carbonated fill foams severely and most craft fillers have never run it.
>
> **ARCH F's 10 g of WPI keeps you inside this problem.** The collagen-only fallback formula gets you out of it. Have both quoted.

**The free move that resolves half of this in a week:** email MONYO, HopTop, Fehér Nyúl and Mad Scientist one Hungarian sentence — ***"Ki dobozol nektek — saját soron vagy mobil szolgáltatóval?"*** ("Who cans for you — your own line or a mobile service?"). Hungarian craft brewing is a small world; three replies will name every canner operating in the country. **This costs nothing and it is the single fastest route to a filler nobody else has found.**

### 5.1 A1 — the powder stick-pack bridge, and why it is genuinely serious

**On EUR 1,000 this is not a consolation prize, it is the only route that exists.** A powder sachet strips out all four risks the can is carrying simultaneously:

| Risk the can carries | Does a sachet carry it? |
|---|---|
| 20 g protein stable and clear at pH 3.2 under 4–5 g/L CO₂ | **No** — the customer adds water |
| Pasteurisation vs preservative hurdle, shelf life in aluminium | **No** — dry powder is ambient-stable 12–24 months by nature |
| Printed can minimums of 150,000–250,000 units | **No** — printed film minimums are far lower |
| DRS registration, 45-day gate, deposit float, EPR on the can | **No** — DRS covers beverage *packaging* 0.1–3 L; a dry sachet is out of scope |

### 5.2 A7 — mobile canning does NOT solve your problem

**A mobile canning truck is a filler + seamer on wheels. It is not a factory.** It arrives, connects to **your** bright tank of finished, carbonated, cold, microbiologically-controlled liquid, and puts it into cans. It brings a counter-pressure filler, a seamer, an operator, usually a depalletiser and lid hopper.

It does **not** bring: (1) **a food-approved production premises** — the canning happens at the *host* site, which must be a registered food establishment; (2) **blending, acidification or pasteurising** — somebody must first make 500–2,000 L of stable pH 3.2 liquid to spec; (3) **a tank**; (4) **cans** — usually the customer supplies them, so you hit the can-purchase MOQ separately; (5) **thermal processing** — without it a carbonated protein soda at pH 3.2 with no preservative is fridge-only for days-to-weeks.

**Therefore mobile canning is never a standalone route. It is "craft brewery contract fill, minus the brewery's own can line."** Pursue it only if you find a brewery or juice producer willing to blend and tank your liquid who has no can line.

**Second structural problem: allergen changeover.** A rig that ran a dairy-protein liquid this morning is at the next brewery this afternoon. Operators are acutely allergen-conscious and several will simply decline dairy protein. **Ask in the first email, not the third.**

**Status: no named Hungarian mobile-canning operator was identified, and no day rate or per-can rate was obtained for any operator in any country.** Fehér Nyúl's own blog states mobile canning lines have arrived in Hungary `LIKELY`. The four-brewery email in §3 resolves this for free.

### 5.3 A9 — self-canning: the honest legal verdict

You asked for this to be priced properly rather than dismissed. Here it is, including what could not be priced.

**The only hard equipment number obtained anywhere:** a used complete can filling line in Germany **from ~EUR 32,000** (machineryline.at listing) `LIKELY`. That is **32× the founder's capital.** A second useful datum: the **Krones Craftmate C**, the smallest *industrial* can filler, is designed around a **minimum batch of 15 hl = 1,500 L ≈ 4,545 × 330 ml** — even "small" industrial equipment assumes a batch bigger than yours. **Bench-top seamer prices, counter-pressure filler prices, carbonation rig prices and seam-gauge prices are all `UNKNOWN - QUOTE REQUIRED`** — no vendor page could be opened, and none has been guessed.

**But the machine price is not the deciding factor. These are, ranked by how fatal:**

1. **SEAM INTEGRITY — this alone ends the discussion.** A double seam *is* the entire food-safety barrier of a can. It is a five-dimensional mechanical structure (body hook, cover hook, overlap, tightness, thickness) that must be **measured and recorded**, not eyeballed. Commercial canneries perform routine seam teardowns at defined intervals every shift and hold the micrometer records. A bench-top seamer with an untrained operator and no seam micrometer produces cans whose safety status is **unknown**. For a **carbonated** product that is a pressure-vessel failure mode; for a **protein** product at pH 3.2 it is a microbiological one. **A hermetically sealed, ambient-stable food is one of the highest-risk product classes in food law — and you would be self-certifying it.**
2. **PREMISES.** A home kitchen will not be approved as an *élelmiszer-előállító üzem* for this product class. You would need rented premises with separated raw/finished areas, washable surfaces, hand-wash stations, verified potable water with periodic testing, pest control, waste handling, temperature control and staff facilities. **Rent + fit-out + deposit alone exceeds the entire budget before a single can is filled.** Note the distinction the "0 HUF" registration figure hides: Hungarian food law separates **bejelentés** (notification — cheap, fast) from **engedélyezés** (authorisation of an inspected production establishment). You would be at the authorisation end. **Procedure, documentation and fee: `UNKNOWN — CONFIRM WITH NÉBIH`.**
3. **HACCP + validated thermal process** — either a validated pasteurisation step (equipment you do not have) or a validated preservative + pH hurdle system, which means paid challenge testing at a lab.
4. **LIABILITY.** **Directive (EU) 2024/2853**, transposing by **2026-12-09**, places own-brand sellers in the manufacturer's liability position. As an actual self-manufacturer you *are* the manufacturer — with, in an egyéni vállalkozó structure, **unlimited personal liability**. A single pressure failure or microbiological incident from an unvalidated seam is an uninsurable, life-altering event.

**VERDICT: NO. Do not self-can for sellable product.** The barrier is premises authorisation and validated seam control, not the seamer's price.

**The one legitimate use:** a bench-top seamer as a **prototyping tool** — make 20 cans, photograph them, hand them to three gym managers, put one in a pitch deck. **Never sell them, never give them to the public, never claim a shelf life.**

## 6. THE EIGHT QUESTIONS THAT GO IN EVERY SINGLE RFQ

Ask all eight in the **first** email. Each round trip costs days, and a filler who answers Q3 with "no dairy on site" saves you the rest.

0. **Can you supply 330 ml SLEEK (Ø ~58 mm), not the standard 66.3 mm "211" body?** — **This is decided: batch 1 is SLEEK**, consistent with the Icelandic benchmark and the sleeve strategy. It drives cans-per-pallet, sleeve dimensions and sleeve artwork, and it is the reason `logistics/23_SHIPPING.md`'s pallet and weight tables (computed on the 66.3 mm standard can) must be recomputed.

1. **Have you filled a >15 g/can protein beverage before? What was the fill-speed penalty?** — 20 g/can in a counter-pressure carbonated fill **foams severely**; most craft/soda fillers have never run it.
2. **Which protein, specifically?** pH 3.2 is correct, not a problem — for the *right* protein. Whey isolate is deliberately run below its isoelectric point (~pH 4.6) to stay clear; that is how every clear-whey product works. Collagen is more forgiving still. **But hydrolysed vs native isolate behave very differently on heat — specify which.**
3. **What is your NÉBIH establishment number, and are dairy raw materials permitted on site?** ⚠️ **THE GATING QUESTION FOR THE 10 g OF WPI.** No dairy establishment approval number was found for any Hungarian filler in this entire research programme. It is completely open.
4. **Thermal process — hot-fill, tunnel pasteurisation, or cold-fill + potassium sorbate?** And what does each do to protein clarity? Cold-fill + sorbate at pH 3.2 is cheapest and may be adequate; tunnel pasteurisation is safer but harder on clarity.
5. **Who supplies the can, and what is the internal lacquer spec?** Low pH + protein + long shelf life needs an appropriate internal coating.
6. **What is your minimum FILLING quantity, and separately, what is your minimum CAN PURCHASE quantity?** ⚠️ **THE CAN MOQ, NOT THE FILL MOQ, IS USUALLY THE BINDING CONSTRAINT.** A filler may happily run 2,000 L and still be unable to buy fewer than a truckload of cans. **This is the most common reason a promising quote collapses.**
7. **Can you supply BLANK cans and apply a SHRINK SLEEVE?** **Never accept a printed-can quote at launch scale** — printed cans are orderable only from **~200,000 pieces in Germany** and **150,000 per variety and order in Austria**; Rhodius's published minimum is 250,000. A sleeve also lets you revise the Hungarian nutrition panel and allergen declaration without scrapping inventory — and a first Hungarian label for a novel functional drink **will** need at least one revision.

**Never open an enquiry with "can you fill 330 ml cans?"** Open with the full matrix — carbonated + 20 g protein + pH 3.2 + thermal process + preservative system. **A "yes" to the wrong question collapses at trial stage and costs weeks.**
