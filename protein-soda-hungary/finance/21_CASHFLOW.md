# 21 — CASHFLOW

**When money leaves and when it comes back.**
Batch 1 = 1,700 cans produced · **60 withheld as samples · 1,640 sellable**
COGS **524 HUF/can** · RRP **899 HUF** · net revenue **707.87 HUF/can** · 1 EUR = 363 HUF

> **You can be profitable on paper and still run out of cash, because you pay for 1,700 cans in week 11 and get paid for them over the following 20 weeks.**

## ⚠️ THE DANGER POINT IS WEEK 11

### **Minimum cash balance: €61 to €261.**

| Case | Sleeve / origination billing | Balance at the trough |
|---|---|---|
| **Good** | Inside the co-packer's per-can price | **€261** |
| **Bad** | Billed separately at the top of the €0–200 range | **€61** |

**€261 is not a buffer on top of your contingency — it IS your contingency.** The €260 contingency line in `18_COMPLETE_STARTUP_COSTS.csv` and this week-11 balance are the same money counted once.

At that moment you own 1,700 cans and almost nothing else. **If anything goes wrong here — a rejected pallet, a freight surcharge, a gym that changes its mind — you have €61 to €261 to react with.**

> 🛑 **Hard instruction, before the deposit is paid:** get the **sleeve/origination cost quoted inside the co-packer's per-can price**, and get the freight quoted **DDP Budapest**. Both are questions, not negotiations, and both are worth €200 each at exactly the moment you have the least money. Do not pay a deposit until you have them in writing.

### Three more ways to protect week 11

1. **Negotiate a 30% deposit instead of 50%.** Ask explicitly. Worst case they say no. It moves €460 from week 7 to week 11 — which does not raise the trough, but it keeps the money available while production runs.
2. **Pre-sell before delivery.** Take deposits from gyms in weeks 7–10 while production runs. **300 cans pre-paid at 799 HUF = 239,700 HUF cash in the door, of which €140 is ÁFA you owe — €520 retained.** That roughly triples the trough.
3. **Do not spend the contingency on stock.** Every euro of it moved into cans is a euro that is not there in week 11.

> ⚠️ **Do not pre-sell at 599 HUF.** 599 gross ÷ 1.27 = **471.65 net against a 524 HUF cost — you lose 52 HUF on every can.** That is the same mistake the wholesale section of this file spends a page warning against. **Pre-sell at 799 minimum** (629.13 net, +105 HUF/can) or take a deposit against the full 899 price. Pre-orders are a cashflow tool, and at 799 they cost you 79 HUF/can of margin versus selling at 899 later — that is the price of the cash, and it is worth paying if the trough is at €61.

## THE SCENARIO WHERE YOU ARE "PROFITABLE" AND STILL FAIL

This is the trap. Read it twice.

You sell **1,640 cans wholesale to 5 gyms at 470 HUF net, on 30-day terms.**

- On paper: revenue 770,800 HUF = **€2,124** against **€2,740** of cash deployed → **a loss of €616**
- Per can: 470 net against a **524 HUF cost = −54 HUF on every can you sell**
- In cash: you paid for the batch in week 11 and receive nothing until week 15+
- If one gym pays late or returns unsold stock, you cannot fund anything else

**Meanwhile the same 1,640 cans sold direct at 899 HUF produce €3,198 and cash arrives weekly.**

> **The channel decision is a cashflow decision AND a margin decision, and both point the same way.** Direct sales pay you immediately at +183.87 HUF/can. Wholesale pays you in 30 days at a price that **loses 54 HUF on every can at this volume**. Wholesale becomes contribution-positive at ~10,000 cans (390 HUF COGS), not before.

## PAYMENT TERMS TO INSIST ON

| Counterparty | Terms to seek |
|---|---|
| **Co-packer** | 30% deposit / 70% on delivery. Never 100% upfront |
| **Gyms (consignment)** | Settle every 2 weeks, or on restock |
| **Gyms (wholesale, later)** | **Prepayment or 8 days** for the first 3 orders. 30 days only after they have reordered twice |
| **Direct/D2C** | Payment before dispatch, always |

> **Never extend 30-day credit to a new gym on your first order.** You cannot afford to finance someone else's business.

## WHAT HAPPENS IF THE BATCH DOES NOT SELL

Honest planning for the likely-enough case. **Net position is after ALL €2,740 of deployed cash — inventory *and* the €440 of setup.** An earlier draft subtracted only the inventory and therefore overstated every row by €440.

| Sold, of 1,640 sellable | Cash recovered @ 899 | **Net position** | Same at 799 RRP |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1,640 (100%)** | €3,198 | **+€458** | +€102 |
| 1,148 (70%) | €2,239 | **−€501** | −€749 |
| 820 (50%) | €1,599 | **−€1,141** | −€1,318 |
| 410 (25%) | €800 | **−€1,940** | −€2,029 |

**The asymmetry is the whole argument for getting LOIs before you order:** selling every can makes €458; selling half loses €1,141. The downside is 2.5× the upside.

### ⚠️ The unsold stock is a write-off, not a carry

`product/05_FOOD_CHEMISTRY.md` and `product/06_SAFETY_AND_SHELF_LIFE.md` both instruct: **print a 6-month best-before on batch 1.** Filled around week 10, that date falls at roughly **week 36**. So:

- The selling window is **weeks 12–36**, and the timeline above deliberately targets **sold out by week 30** to leave six weeks of margin.
- **Anything still in the garage at the date is worth zero**, not "recoverable if you keep selling". The 70% / 50% / 25% rows above are final positions, not snapshots.
- An earlier version of this file assumed a **9–12 month** best-before and ran the timeline to week 34. Both were wrong: it contradicted the project's own product files, and it valued dead stock as live stock.

> 🛑 **Ask every co-packer for the shelf life in the first email, and get the thermal process in writing.** If the route is cold-fill with no pasteurisation, the real shelf life may be **days to weeks under refrigeration** `product/06 §6.4` — in which case this table is optimistic by an order of magnitude and the gym-fridge model does not work at all.
